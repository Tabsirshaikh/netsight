# Sonar Confidence Maps Still Lack Buyers

**Verdict: do not build a company around this yet. Test it for eight weeks first.** As currently framed (India first, side-scan/SAS review with calibrated per-object confidence and a search-completeness map), the research found no buyer who is shown to pay for it, in India or abroad. The global survey economy is real: hydrographic equipment is reported at roughly USD 2.5-4.1 bn and Fugro's Marine division alone had about EUR 1.75 bn revenue in 2024, but those figures are low-reliability market-research numbers or search summaries, and side-scan hardware is only tens to a few hundred million USD. India is the weakest place to start: the only fully verified port survey contract was about Rs 33 lakh over three years for third-party witnessing of dredging bathymetry, the Navy surveys in-house, and the offshore-wind auctions that would create UXO and boulder work were cancelled in 2025. The best-evidenced pain is outside India: only about 3-8% of investigated offshore UXO targets turn out to be real, and every false target costs vessel time. But the money is in magnetometer and multibeam target lists, contractors such as Coda Octopus and Ocean Geophysics already bundle AI detection into their products and services, and no one has been shown paying separately for calibrated confidence or coverage. The most plausible niche is independent assurance of offshore-wind and cable target lists (a calibrated "how far to trust this list and what was actually searched" report), sold globally from an India or Europe base, with search-and-recovery contractors as a second, lumpier customer. The likeliest good outcome is a small specialist business or a licence to a sonar or software vendor, not a venture-scale company; no civilian pure-play AI sonar-review firm with disclosed revenue or a disclosed-price exit turned up. What is verified, what is inference, and what is unknown is marked throughout, and the plan in the last section is built to kill the idea cheaply if the buyer does not exist.

## Survey services are a multi-billion market, but sonar review software is a thin slice

Start with what can be trusted. No independent, primary-sourced figure exists for "sonar survey services," and the market-research firms disagree by one to two orders of magnitude depending on scope. Hydrographic survey equipment clusters around USD 2.5-4.1 bn for 2025 at 5-7% growth, for example Expert Market Research at about USD 3.65 bn ([Expert Market Research](https://expertmarketresearch.com/reports/hydrographic-survey-equipment-market)) and Marketpublishers at USD 4.1 bn ([Marketpublishers](https://marketpublishers.com/report/vehicle/sea_transport/hydrographic-survey-equipment-market-og.html)), while Spherical Insights says USD 7.41 bn ([Spherical Insights](https://www.sphericalinsights.com/blogs/top-25-companies-in-global-hydrographic-survey-equipment-market-2026-2035-spherical-insights-analysis)) and Report Prime says USD 1.06 bn ([Report Prime](https://www.reportprime.com/hydrographic-survey-system-r13039.md)). Side-scan sonar alone is reported at USD 44-50 m by three firms ([Global Info Research](https://www.marketresearch.com/GlobalInfoResearch-v4117/Global-Side-Scan-Sonars-Company-41432729), [DIResearch](https://www.marketresearch.com/Deep-Insights-Research-Co-DIR-v4285/Global-Side-Scan-Sonars-Research-41780036)) and USD 275 m by another ([Credence Research](https://www.credenceresearch.com/report/side-scan-sonars-market)). Marine survey services show USD 4.75-5.18 bn ([Research and Markets](https://www.researchandmarkets.com/reports/6055294/marine-survey-services-market-global-forecast)). All of these are paywalled or reseller-page figures read through search summaries; one listing even shows a 2025 value of "$7.3 million," an obvious unit error, and a "marine survey for subsea cables" figure of USD 4.2 bn exceeds TeleGeography's entire cable-construction spend of about USD 2 bn a year ([TeleGeography](https://blog.telegeography.com/building-tomorrows-internet-an-update-on-new-cable-investment)), so it is not credible. Treat the equipment cluster as "a few billion dollars, and mostly not sonar," and treat any pitch-deck TAM built from these as decoration.

The one real-money anchor is Fugro. Its Marine revenue was EUR 1,753.7 m in 2024 and fell 15.5% (currency-comparable) in 2025, with nine-month Marine revenue of EUR 1,097.3 m against EUR 1,304.0 m a year earlier, and renewables dropped from 38% to 26% of group revenue as offshore wind slowed ([Baird Maritime](https://www.bairdmaritime.com/amp/story/offshore/fugros-2025-profits-impacted-by-offshore-wind-slowdown), [Fugro Annual Report 2025](https://a.storyblok.com/f/329380/x/246cdb971b/fugro-annual-report-2025.pdf)). The full-year Marine figure for 2025 was not confirmed because the annual report could not be read. The inference that matters for a student team is that the best-evidenced pain segment is also the most cyclical one, and that survey spend is a small line inside very large projects: geodata is put at 1-4% of offshore-wind project cost by Fugro ([Fugro deck](https://netzeroatlantic.ca/sites/default/files/2025-10/KSmith_Fugro_NovaScotia_RD_Forum_09242025.pdf)). Software that trims part of that line has a ceiling, and nobody has measured the share of survey cost that is interpretation labour; that number was not found for any segment.

Who actually pays, by segment, is clearer than how much. Offshore-wind developers and cable consortia pay survey contractors (Fugro, Gardline-type firms, N-Sea, Bibby HydroMap, Ocean Infinity) for site, boulder, UXO and route surveys. Navies buy mine-countermeasures systems from primes: the US Navy's Textron MCM USV software and integration contract is about USD 100.1 m, probably a ceiling with about USD 4.7 m obligated at award ([Orange Slices](https://orangeslices.ai/?p=125378), [Naval News](https://www.navalnews.com/naval-news/2025/02/u-s-navy-awards-contracts-for-deployment-of-future-mcm-capabilities/)), and the buyer wants certified, ATR-integrated kit from Thales, SeeByte, Raytheon and Kongsberg, which a student startup will not win directly. Governments buy search-and-recovery rarely and as outcomes: Malaysia's 2025-26 MH370 deal with Ocean Infinity paid USD 70 m only on a find, covered about 15,000 km2, and ended without findings so no fee was paid ([SCMP](https://www.scmp.com/news/asia/southeast-asia/article/3303034/malaysia-agrees-no-find-no-fee-terms-restart-flight-mh370-search), [Australian Aviation](https://australianaviation.com.au/2026/03/no-luck-for-ocean-infinity-in-latest-hunt-for-mh370/)). Ghost-gear sonar work is funded by small grants: a USD 8 m four-year NOAA-funded programme with sub-awards such as USD 133,172 for a side-scan survey ([EurekAlert](https://www.eurekalert.org/news-releases/1103691)), and awards of USD 0.13-5 m elsewhere. Port, dredging, oil-and-gas, archaeology and insurance spend per project were not found, so those segments cannot be ranked from evidence.

## India buys small, in-house or hardware-first, and no tender for side-scan review turned up

The Indian evidence is a sample of individual tenders, not a census: the GeM and CPPP portals could not be queried, IWAI's site was unreachable, and several port PDFs returned the wrong content. With that limit stated, here is what was found. Ports do tender hydrographic work, but small and bathymetry-centred. New Mangalore Port Authority's tender for a third party to witness and certify pre- and post-dredging hydrographic surveys had an estimated value of **Rs 32,87,847 for three years (about Rs 11 lakh a year, roughly USD 12,000-13,000 at my conversion)**, an eligibility floor of Rs 9.9 lakh average turnover, and a scope of verifying the dredging contractor's own survey equipment calibration and certifying its charts ([NMPA tender PDF](https://newmangaloreport.gov.in/index.php/sites/default/files/2023-07/HydrographicSurveyTD.pdf)); this one was opened and read. V.O. Chidambaranar Port's September 2025 bathymetry tender was estimated at Rs 50,16,500 and reportedly includes a DGPS side-scan sonar and survey boat for dredging monitoring, but that rests on a search summary because the PDF would not open ([VOC Port NIT 96](https://www.vocport.gov.in/VOCPORT_HINDI/port/UserInterface/DailyVessel/Engg(Civil)_20250926184439_NIT_96.pdf)). Paradip appears to run an in-house Hydrographic Survey Division and hire assistance ([TenderDetail listing](https://www.tenderdetail.com/Indian-Tenders/TenderNotice/26075733/1b558882c9d701c6853c2833e85fc2eb)), Deendayal Port issued an April 2024 EOI for channel and berth-pocket surveys ([Deendayal EOI](https://www.deendayalport.gov.in/wp-content/uploads/2024/04/EOI-3.pdf)), and Kolkata tendered to buy its own echo-sounder ([Tender18](https://www.tender18.com/tenders-details/15412965)); all three are search-summary only. The inference is that a single port's recurring survey budget is in the lakhs of rupees, and a dozen major ports together are plausibly a few crore a year in bathymetry (my back-of-envelope, not a sourced figure). A software product that takes a slice of a slice cannot be a company on port tenders alone.

IWAI is a recurring outsourcing buyer, but of river bathymetry. Its open tenders cover a detailed hydrographic survey of NW-2 (Neamatighat to Dibrugarh) and monthly thalweg surveys in Maharashtra, Gujarat, Goa and Karnataka, with one secondary source putting the programme at about 10,000 km of longitudinal survey a month ([TenderShark](https://tendershark.com/authority/inland-waterways-authority-of-india-tenders/active), [Geospatial World](https://geospatialworld.net/article/surveying-and-mapping-of-national-waterways), [Shankar IAS explainer](https://www.shankariasparliament.com/current-affairs/navigating-indias-waterways)). No contract value or winner was found, and echo-sounder depth lines are not side-scan target interpretation. The Navy and National Hydrographic Office are the opposite case: INHD's own report to the IHO lists six ocean-going survey ships plus a catamaran, customers including "Commercial," and in-house processing ([INHD national report](https://iho.int/uploads/user/Inter-Regional%20Coordination/RHC/SAIHC/SAIHC19/SAIHC19-7.11_2023_India%20National%20Report.pdf)), and the four Sandhayak-class ships cost **Rs 2,435.15 crore** under a 2018 contract, with the fourth delivered in March 2026 ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sandhayak-class_survey_vessel_(2023)), a secondary source). The Navy is a sensor buyer with its own analysts; data access for a civilian startup is likely restricted, though that point is unsourced. No NHO outsourcing contract or civil data-sale policy was found.

The research bodies and energy companies spend more but buy hardware and large survey packages. A NIOT tender-results row of Rs 2,49,61,234 to Deekay Marine Services may be a side-scan system, but the attribution is unverified ([NIOT results](https://niot.res.in/media/Tender%20Results%20for%20the%20month%20of%20August%202024/Jan%20%202025%2020250213181414.pdf)). Fugro ran a roughly 56,000 line-km bathymetric survey for NIOT off Andhra Pradesh and won ONGC's fourth consecutive three-year integrated survey contract at about USD 7.7 m, plus a USD 26 m ONGC geotechnical contract ([Hydro International](https://www.hydro-international.com/content/article/fugro-commences-renewed-ongc-survey-contract-offshore-india), [OGJ](https://www.ogj.com/general-interest/article/14183369/ongc-lets-integrated-survey-services-contract-to-fugro)), all search-summary values that vary by source. Disaster forces buy ROV hardware in single units: an NDRF EOI for an ROV with sonar ([NDRF](https://ndrf.gov.in/sites/default/files/EoI%20GeM%20Bid%20No%207530906%20for%20Under%20Water%20ROV%20with%20SONAR%20along%20with%20Draft%20Technical%20Specifications%20and%20Trial%20Directives%20of%20Under%20Water%20ROV.pdf)) and Gandhinagar Municipal Corporation's 2026 ROV tender with no stated cost ([DeshGujarat](https://deshgujarat.com/2026/03/10/gandhinagar-civic-body-to-buy-underwater-rov-for-search-rescue-ops/)). During the 2024 Shirur landslide search, the district proposed a Rs 96 lakh dredger while Navy sonar flagged a large object that took 72 days and several wrong leads to resolve ([Kerala Kaumudi](https://keralakaumudi.com/en/news/news.php?id=1371983), [Onmanorama](https://www.onmanorama.com/news/kerala/2024/09/25/72-days-search-arjun-timeline-events-kozhikode-truck-body-gangavali.html)); that shows episodic pain with tens of lakhs at stake and no sonar-software budget line.

Offshore wind, the segment that drives the global pain case, is stalled in India. SECI cancelled 4.5 GW of offshore-wind tenders in August 2025, Rs 7,453 crore of viability-gap funding exists for a first 1 GW whose tender the ministry had not decided to launch as of March 2026, and NIWE's own survey was reported complete ([Down To Earth](https://www.downtoearth.org.in/energy/india-reconsiders-1-gw-offshore-wind-tender-after-earlier-auctions-fail-to-attract-bidders-official-tells-dte)). NIWE's 2023 Gulf of Mannar investigation tender shows no mention of side-scan, UXO or boulders ([Mercom](https://mercomindia.com/niwe-floats-tender-for-offshore-tamil-nadu)). Cable landings are happening (Airtel's SEA-ME-WE 6, Meta's Waterworth via Sify) but no marine route-survey contracts for Indian waters were found ([DT Next](https://www.dtnext.in/amp/story/news/business/airtel-completes-landing-of-sea-me-we-6-submarine-cable-in-chennai-823354), [Inc42](https://inc42.com/buzz/sify-technologies-to-connect-india-to-metas-waterworth-project)), and consortia typically hire international survey firms, so reach would be indirect (inference). The private-sector benchmark is Planys, which raised Rs 100 crore in December 2025 but sells ROVs, inspection services and defence UUVs, not sonar AI ([CXOToday](https://cxotoday.com/media-coverage/planys-raises-₹100-crore-to-accelerate-global-expansion-and-launch-defence-arm-planys-ark/)); its revenue is reported at Rs 12.8 crore and Rs 15.7 crore in two panels of the same Inc42 page, so use "a few to about 15 crore" and nothing finer ([Inc42](https://inc42.com/company/planys-technologies/financials/)). Other Indian survey firms (Geoscience, Ocean Data Services, Deep Sea Technologies, Seabed Surveys, Aquasurvey, Geomarine) returned no public customer or revenue data.

What was not found matters as much. There was no Indian tender for a wreck, obstruction or side-scan-only survey, no port award values or winners, no frequency count across ports, nothing relevant from the Coast Guard, NIO or INCOIS, and no Indian agency, regulator or insurer asking for a quantified coverage metric. The SIH26057 problem statement could not be found, so its link to any procurement is unknown. The funding channels that do exist are real but defence-shaped: the Deep Ocean Mission is about Rs 4,077 crore over five years ([MoES](https://www.moes.gov.in/sites/default/files/RS-in-English-978-10022022.pdf)), and iDEX challenges have funded sonar and AI work, for example a 3D forward-looking sonar challenge and AI noise cancellation for sonars in Navy DISC-7 ([iDEX DISC-7](https://idex.gov.in/uploads/notice/indian%20navy%20result%20disc%207%20Updated%20as%20of%2029th%20Sep_0.pdf)), but no open call for civil survey QA was found. The honest summary: India offers grant money and a cheap place to build, not a paying customer for this product today.

## UXO false alarms are the only quantified pain, and no one is shown paying for confidence

Offshore UXO is where the numbers are. The GEOMAR/BASTA paper states that large numbers of false-positive targets "drive costs and efforts" and that most investigated objects are scrap or boulders ([GEOMAR](https://oceanrep.geomar.de/id/eprint/55572/1/TacklingCostDriversofMaritimeEXOSurveys.pdf)). Named projects put the real-UXO share at about 8% (Dudgeon: 243 potential targets, 20 needing clearance), 3% (a Boskalis Baltic cable campaign: 773 targets, 25 UXO) and about 6% (SeaTerra, 2026), and ALM Geophysics reports about 4% across 18 campaigns ([Renewable Energy World](https://www.renewableenergyworld.com/?p=206652), [SWZ Maritime](https://swzmaritime.nl/news/2024/01/23/how-boskalis-searches-the-seabed-for-unexploded-ordnance/), [BSPC](https://resources.bspc.net/documents/utf-8Q20260517C2AD5FBSPC5Fv02pdf2.pdf), [Seequent/ALM](https://www.seequent.com/optimizing-uxo-target-lists-with-alm-geophysics-library-of-previously-found-targets/)). These are better evidence than Fugro's marketing claim that "up to 99%" or "95%" of targets are false positives, which is internally inconsistent in the same article ([Fugro](https://www.fugro.com/news/long-reads/2021/five-key-uxo-considerations-for-successful-offshore-projects-fugro-longread)). The price signal is vessel-days: ALM cites a vessel above GBP 50,000 a day investigating about 10 targets a day, and Fugro says 1,500 targets need three times the vessel days of 500. My arithmetic combining the two (different campaigns, both vendors, so illustrative only) is roughly GBP 5,000 per investigated target, about 150 vessel days and GBP 7.5 m for a 1,500-target list, and roughly 15 days or GBP 750,000 saved by a 10% cut. Delay costs dwarf this, though they are unrelated to sonar contacts: Dominion reported a stop-work cost of about USD 5 m a day on one project ([gCaptain](https://gcaptain.com/federal-work-halt-and-tariffs-push-dominions-offshore-wind-project-to-11-5b/), search summary).

Three caveats cut this pain down to size for a side-scan product. First, UXO target lists come mostly from magnetometer and multibeam work, not side-scan; the GEOMAR neural-network target picker worked on multibeam, flagged objects that were probably not UXO, and still needed AUV magnetometer validation. A sonar-only tool addresses a part of the list. Second, no source gives a cost per verified target from an independent or primary source, no UXO spread day rate, and no analyst hours per line-km, so the GBP 5,000 figure is derived, not found. Third, the cost-bearer and the cost-saver may differ: if contractors are paid by the day, fewer investigations reduce their revenue unless the developer demands it (inference, untested). The Carbon Trust's guidance concluded that the survey problem was design rather than equipment, and warned that errors can force repeat surveys, which suggests the hook is "defensible survey design and proof" rather than "faster picking" ([Windtech](https://www.windtech-international.com/industry-news/new-guidance-for-offshore-wind-industry-on-geophysical-surveys-for-unexploded-ordnance-and-boulders)).

On manual-review burden the evidence is qualitative and vendor-sourced. The Hydro International 2025 industry survey puts skilled-staff availability as the most common theme, consultants see automation as an answer, and hydrographers expect AI to work alongside, not replace, experienced staff ([Hydro International](https://www.hydro-international.com/content/article/the-pressure-beneath-the-surface), search summary). The strongest speed claim is Coda Octopus's: 4,600 boulder contacts in 3 km of line data in 9 minutes, versus about three days manually, at a 91% detection rate against manual tagging, on a customer trial with unnamed customers ([Ocean News](https://www.oceannews.com/k46h)). That is a vendor claim, and it also shows the feature is being given away inside bundles. Ocean Geophysics says its boulder AI "will not be sold stand-alone" ([Offshore Engineer](https://www.oedigital.com/news/512069-ai-tool-to-tackle-boulder-issues-for-offshore-wind-developments)). Hours per line-km, backlog length and cost per line-km were not found anywhere.

Does anyone pay for confidence or coverage today? People pay for assurance, but sold as a human or organisational certificate, not as computed probabilities. The nearest Indian example is the NMPA tender above, where a port pays an independent party to witness and certify its dredging survey. Offshore, Fugro sells an ALARP certificate with a single mobilisation fee and says no price ([Fugro](https://www.fugro.com/news/long-reads/2021/five-key-uxo-considerations-for-successful-offshore-projects-fugro-longread)); IHO S-44 requires a "full sea-floor search" capable of detecting 1 m (Special Order) or 2 m (Order 1a) cubes, a binary specification verified by cube trials ([IHO C-13](https://docs.iho.int/iho_pubs/CB/C-13/english/C-13_Chapter_4.pdf)); specialist professional-indemnity cover for hydrographic surveyors exists ([ITIC](https://www.itic-insure.com/fileadmin/uploads/itic/Documents/Factsheets/2026/Hydrographic_surveyors_fact_sheet_2026.pdf)). Search-and-recovery pays for area covered and success, with the contractor carrying coverage risk, so a coverage and Pd tool is worth more to the contractor than to the government (inference). What was not found: any buyer paying for a calibrated per-object probability, any regulator or insurer that accepts or discounts for a probabilistic coverage claim, any missed-object claim value, any insurance premium link, or any documented case of a side-scan "channel clear" declaration followed by a strike. The missed-object cost is probably large per event and rare, but there is no frequency or claim data.

The ranking is my judgement from the sources above; the Indian segments (port channel clearance, post-disaster, ghost nets) do not make the top tier because no budgeted buyer was found. For ghost gear, Mongabay (June 2026) finds India has no systematic tracking or retrieval, some state and NGO schemes pay fishers Rs 15,000-70,000 per entangled-animal release or net return, CSR funding is "unreliable," and sonar is not mentioned ([Mongabay](https://news.mongabay.com/2026/06/indias-fishers-confront-homegrown-ghost-gear-problem/)). **The Stolte et al. 2022 cost figures (EUR 140 per hectare of diver verification and 5 diving days for 49 positions) could not be verified and should be removed from any pitch.** Several searches failed to find them, two candidate Frontiers 2022 papers that were opened (Bell et al.; Chatzievangelou et al.) contain neither figure, and the earlier fetch of Stolte's paper (Frontiers 9:981840) covered only the first 100,000 of about 114,000 characters and surfaced neither number ([Frontiers](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fmars.2022.981840)). That paper does support 549 suspects, 176 diver-verified and 94 confirmed (53%, reported as 52%), over 5,820 ha in 45 days, with no miss rate reported. Until the figures are located in the full text, the dive-cost claim in the NetSight proposal has no source.

## Incumbents bundle AI, consolidation is the exit, and the integration door is narrow

The competitor picture is crowded at the feature level and thin at the "trust layer" level. Detection and ATR already ship or are announced from many sources: Kongsberg's SAS Target Assistant (built with Cathx Ocean) produces a target list "for review/confirmation" with no published confidence or false-alarm figures ([Kongsberg](https://www.kongsberg.com/news/news-archive/2024/sas-target-assistant/)); Chesapeake's SonarWiz 8.1 advertises CNN-based ATR ([Sea Technology](https://sea-technology.com/sonarwiz-8-1-0)) and a February 2026 article says its models are "being developed" to cut false-alarm review "by an order of magnitude" with detections that "carry probability estimates," giving no calibration method ([Maritime Magazines](https://www.maritimemagazines.com/marine-technology/202602/the-future-of-sonar-processing)); Charles River's AutoTRap claims 90% Pd on one Icelandic seabed ([Ocean Science & Technology](https://www.oceansciencetechnology.com/feature/charles-river-analytics-maritime-autonomy-technologies/)); EvoLogics, FarSounder (state grant to add AI, Jan 2026), Exail, Thales (MiMap "up to 4x faster," a vendor claim) and SeeByte (AI detection and terrain-aware performance mapping, per its 2024 arXiv paper) fill the defence side ([Ocean News](https://oceannews.com/news/science-technology/farsounder-awarded-manufacturing-innovation-voucher-to-advance-ai-based-sonar-capabilities/), [Thales](https://www.thalesgroup.com/en/solutions-catalogue/defence/naval/advanced-sonar-data-analysis), [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.18663v1)). In UXO, Seequent/ALM sell target-list optimisation and Bibby HydroMap built its own boulder CNN with STFC Hartree ([STFC Hartree](https://www.hartree.stfc.ac.uk/case-studies/2020/01/09/boulder-detection-from-seabed-sonar-images/)). Two notes disagree on Kongsberg: the pain-point researcher found no product called "Target Assistant," while the earlier research read the 2024 press release at the link above; the release is the stronger evidence, so it stays, flagged. The pattern across all of them is bundling, which compresses stand-alone pricing: MIND Technology licensed its SpectralAi side-scan suite to General Oceans after Klein was sold ([BlueZone Group](https://bluezonegroup.com.au/announcements/mind-technology-spectralai-software)), which shows ATR being treated as a licensable asset rather than a product line.

The funded startups show the same shape. The closest 2026 comparator is HydroSight (Haifa), a $5 m seed announced 7 September 2026 for software that fuses multi-source, multi-epoch survey data and flags changes for ports, energy and cable owners; it says it runs projects with unnamed infrastructure owners and is too new to show outcomes ([Calcalist](https://www.calcalistech.com/ctechnews/article/syzscx2oge)). Terradepth has raised about USD 28.35 m over eight rounds (CB Insights, search summary) and launched its Absolute Ocean cloud platform in November 2024 before steering it toward Navy prototype agreements in 2026 ([OE Digital](https://www.oedigital.com/amp/news/519273-terradepth-launches-cloud-based-ocean-mapping-tool-for-offshore-energy-firms), [BusinessWire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20260409951116/en/)); Bedrock Ocean raised a USD 25 m Series A-2 in June 2025 but runs its own AUVs and sells data services ([Built In](https://builtin.com/articles/bedrock-ocean-raises-25m-20250611)). Greensea IQ lives on US Navy and Marine Corps contracts, for example an USD 18.15 m sole-source IDIQ reported July 2026 ([Unmanned Systems Technology](https://www.unmannedsystemstechnology.com/2026/07/greensea-iq-awarded-contract-for-subsea-autonomy-controller-development/)). The failures are instructive: Rovco raised almost GBP 50 m, merged with an AI firm, posted losses above GBP 8 m a year and entered administration ([Business Cloud](https://businesscloud.co.uk/news/rovco-administration-to-make-100-staff-redundant/)); Nauticus Robotics carries going-concern doubt on aggregator figures, not primary filings ([Panabee](https://www.panabee.com/news/nauticus-robotics-earnings-q2-2026-report)). Ghost-gear AI is NGO- and grant-funded: GhostNetZero.ai (WWF Germany, Microsoft, Accenture) and GEOMAR/sonoware's GhostNetBusters (a EUR 221k project) are not revenue businesses ([Microsoft Research](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/?p=1150234), [sonoware](https://sonoware.de/news/2024-08-28_foerderzusage_ghostnetbusters)), and Ocean AID's plan to license to sonar firms by 2026 shows no sign of delivery ([Caplight](https://www.caplight.com/company/oceanaid)).

For exits, the verified precedents are acquisitions by hardware or platform players, none with a disclosed price for a pure AI-sonar-review company. SeeByte was bought by Bluefin Robotics in 2013 at an undisclosed price and kept its platform-agnostic products ([OE Digital](https://www.oedigital.com/news/457500-bluefin-robotics-acquires-seebyte)). Kraken Robotics bought Covelya Group (Sonardyne, EIVA, Voyis, Wavefront) for C$615 m, closed 2 July 2026, citing defence and maritime surveillance as the rationale ([Barchart](https://www.barchart.com/story/news/545088/kraken-robotics-announces-signing-of-strategic-acquisition-to-expand-global-maritime-capabilities)). That makes the owner of EIVA's survey software, Sonardyne and Wavefront a defence-oriented consolidator: a candidate channel or acquirer, and a reason integration may get harder (inference). The inference from this section: independent civilian AI-review software has no venture-scale precedent; survivors earn government contract flow or are absorbed.

Integration is the practical constraint. HYPACK's SDK is not publicly downloadable, access is by request and can be denied or deferred, owning a HYPACK licence does not entitle SDK access, selling or bundling needs a separate written agreement, and "SDK materials may not be used to train or fine-tune AI or machine learning systems" ([Xylem HYPACK](https://www.xylem.com/en-qa/brand/hypack/integrate-with-hypack/)). No public SonarWiz plug-in API, and no QPS, CARIS or EIVA plug-in terms, were found. Pricing is norm-setting but opaque: EIVA support is 15% of list per year after the first, HYPACK offers lease with 85% credit toward purchase, SonarWiz is free to universities and discounted up to 35% in bundles, and the only CARIS figure, USD 110, is unexplained ([EIVA](https://eiva.com/products/licence-types-and-discounts), [Xylem](https://www.xylem.com/siteassets/brand/hypack/resources/newsletter/2020/4-october/hypack-licensing-lease-or-buy-boris-schulze.pdf), [Ocean News](https://oceannews.com/?p=43353), [CARIS](https://www.teledynecaris.com/en/products/hips-sips/hips-sips-essential)). Inference: the lowest-friction path is a standalone tool that reads exported XTF/JSF/GeoTIFF and writes vendor-neutral GeoJSON, shapefile or CSV, keeping training data independent of any SDK material; the cost is weak workflow lock-in. Data-format openness for XTF and JSF was not verified in this research and should be confirmed.

## Verdict: do not build yet; the best odds are target-list assurance for offshore wind and cable work

**Should the team build a startup now? No.** The honest reading of every note is that the problem is real, the gap in documented calibration and coverage is probably real, and the buyer is unproven. The team should treat this as a hypothesis worth eight weeks of cheap testing, not a company to incorporate. The India-first plan fails on three verified facts: ports spend lakhs on bathymetry, the Navy and NHO process in-house, and offshore-wind site surveys are deferred while auctions are reworked. India still has legitimate roles as a place to get data (NIOT, port ROV firms, Planys-type contractors), to find grant funding (iDEX, Deep Ocean Mission, IIT Madras incubation), and to build cheaply. It is a poor place to expect first revenue from this specific product.

**Which niche has the best odds?** Independent, auditable assurance for offshore-wind and cable geophysical target lists, delivered as a report rather than a plug-in: calibrated reliability for each contact, a plain coverage and Pd map showing what the survey could and could not have seen, and an explicit false-alarm budget. The reasons are the only quantified false-alarm pain found, an identifiable budget holder (developers, TSOs and contractors bidding on UXO ALARP evidence), the regulatory vocabulary of "full search" and ALARP, and no vendor documenting validated calibration. The reasons for caution are equally concrete: demand is cyclical, sonar is only part of the list, and contractors bundle AI. The second candidate is a coverage-and-Pd product for search-and-recovery contractors, where "how well was each area searched" is the core question and the MH370 contracts show coverage claims matter; budgets are episodic (inference), so it is better as credibility and a demo than as the revenue base. Defence MCM, ghost gear and Indian ports are the three to deprioritise. A final point on positioning: hydrographers say AI should assist, not replace, so position as "second opinion with evidence," not automation.

**What would have to be true.** Interviews would need to show that (1) some buyer owns a budget for target-list QA or coverage proof and has paid for something like it, even by hand; (2) calibrated probabilities change a decision they already make, such as which targets to investigate or whether a re-survey is needed; (3) real contractors will share even anonymised data so the calibration can be demonstrated on real, not only injected, targets; (4) a vendor-neutral file-in, report-out workflow is acceptable; and (5) a price of at least a few thousand dollars per survey or a five-figure annual licence is realistic, because below that a team of students cannot sustain support. None of these five has any direct evidence yet.

**What is not known.** There is no willingness-to-pay data for any AI sonar-review product; no hours-per-line-km, backlog or analyst-share-of-cost figure; no cost per verified target, spread day rate or insurer link; no tender frequency for Indian side-scan or wreck surveys; no port award values; no market size for sonar processing software; and whether any regulator accepts probabilistic coverage claims. The research also did not search oil and gas, dredging, archaeology and insurance buyers individually, and defence sales-cycle length was not quantified. Absence of evidence here comes from limited web searching, mostly through search summaries, and is not proof of absence; internal naval and contractor tools may already do part of this.

## Eight weeks of interviews and three cheap tests decide it

The customer-discovery plan below targets the five unknowns above. Aim for 20-25 conversations in eight weeks, with at least half outside India, since the strongest pain is global. Lead with the buyer's last real survey, not with the product.

| Segment | Who to interview (organisation types, named where the research found them) | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Offshore-wind and cable survey contractors, global | UXO and geophysical data-processing leads at Fugro, N-Sea, Ocean Geophysics, Bibby HydroMap, Ocean Infinity, Gardline-type firms; UXO specialists such as 6 Alpha and Ordtek | Owners of target-list review hours and false-alarm cost |
| Developers, TSOs and their QA consultants | Geoscience and UXO leads at offshore-wind developers and TSOs that issue UXO tenders (50Hertz and Elia were named in the notes), marine warranty surveyors | Likely budget holders for assurance and ALARP evidence |
| Insurers and standards bodies | ITIC and other hydrographic professional-indemnity underwriters, cable operators, IHO or national hydrographic-office QA staff | Test whether coverage proof earns discounts or acceptance |
| Software and sensor gatekeepers | Chesapeake (SonarWiz), Xylem (HYPACK), QPS, EIVA/Kraken, General Oceans/Klein, EdgeTech, EvoLogics | Channel, API, price benchmarks and acquirer interest |
| Indian ports and inland waterways | Hydrographic or marine departments at NMPA, VOC, Paradip, Deendayal, Chennai-area ports; IWAI regional hydrographic units | Test frequency, budget and what "clear" means in practice |
| Indian survey and research bodies | NIOT acoustics and ocean-tech groups, NCCR, NCPOR, GSI marine wing, small private survey firms that win port tenders, Planys, Pan India Consultants, the ONGC and Reliance survey desks, Fugro Survey India | Data access, analyst hours, subcontract routes |
| Disaster and search-and-recovery | NDRF and state disaster forces, Coast Guard contacts, Ocean Infinity, Navy contacts reached via iDEX mentors | Test episodic demand and coverage-claim value |
| Peers | HydroSight, Bedrock, Terradepth, the GhostNetZero/WWF Germany and GEOMAR/sonoware teams | Positioning and channel lessons |

Ask the same eight questions in every interview, and write down the answers verbatim. The confirm and kill thresholds are my judgement, set so a few clear no's are enough to stop.

| Question | Confirms the idea if | Kills the idea if |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Walk me through the last survey you reviewed: who reviewed it, hours per line-km, what it cost, what the backlog was. | They give hours or cost and call review a bottleneck on at least some projects | Review is a small cost or "we do not have a backlog" in most interviews |
| 2. How do you prove to a client or regulator that an area was fully searched? What went wrong with that proof? | They name a concrete failure, rework, dispute or lost bid | A mosaic and a signed statement are always accepted |
| 3. What did false targets cost on your last UXO or boulder campaign, in vessel days or money? | They quote numbers or a recent example and would pay to cut it | Target counts are fixed by contract, or the contractor is paid per target |
| 4. What do you use for AI or ATR today, and what do you not trust about it? | They name a tool and distrust its scores or lack of coverage evidence | Their incumbent tool is "good enough" and bundled |
| 5. What would you need to see before trusting an AI "no target here"? Would you share anonymised data to test it? | They specify evidence (reliability diagram, Pd on your data) and agree to share | Nobody shares data or evidence will not move them |
| 6. Who signs off the budget, and from which line (project, overhead, client-mandated)? | A named role and budget line exist | "Nobody owns this" or it is done free by the surveyor |
| 7. Would you use a tool outside your main software (export/import), or only a plug-in? | A file-based workflow is acceptable | Only an integrated plug-in is acceptable (and the SDK is gated) |
| 8. What would you pay per survey, per seat or per year, and what is the procurement path? | A price in the thousands of dollars per survey or five figures per year, with a path under 6 months | Price answers are token, or procurement needs a multi-year framework |

Three experiments should run before writing any more code. The first is a hand-made "wizard of oz" assurance report: take a public survey (BenthiCat or AI4Shipwrecks) and produce, with existing open tools and spreadsheet effort, a three-page PDF with a reliability diagram for an off-the-shelf detector, a Pd map from injected test targets and a list of red re-survey zones, then show it in interviews and ask what in it they would act on. Success is two or more practitioners naming a specific decision it would change; failure is "interesting, but we never get asked for this." The second is a 30-day tender and price census: set keyword alerts and do manual searches on GeM, CPPP and port portals for "side scan," "wreck survey," "obstruction survey" and "hydrographic" to count real Indian tenders (the research could not query these portals), and collect three reseller quotes for SonarWiz, HYPACK and Qimera to anchor price. Success is a recurring flow of side-scan or obstruction tenders at crore-scale; failure is fewer than a handful a year, all bathymetry. The third is a paid or near-paid pilot ask: offer a free retrospective audit of one anonymised survey in exchange for review-hour data, a written price reaction and a letter of intent. Success is at least two organisations sharing real data, one giving a price and one signing a letter of intent; failure is no one sharing data in eight weeks. If experiments one and three both fail, stop. If they succeed, the next spend is a proper calibration study on real labelled data, not a dashboard.

## Conclusion

The research changes the question. The team started from "is the problem real?" and the answer is yes but beside the point: the problem is real in UXO and survey assurance, where a verified 3-8% hit rate makes false alarms expensive, yet the thing the team would sell, a validated statement of how far to trust a contact and what was searched, has no buyer on the record. Everyone pays for assurance as a human certificate, an ALARP letter or an outcome contract, and incumbents give detection away. The venture-scale question is therefore about whether the calibrated evidence layer can become the thing a developer or contractor must show to proceed, which is a sales-and-standards question as much as a modelling one.

Two uncomfortable implications follow. India is a good place to prove the evidence and a poor place to expect the first cheque, so a plan that leads with Indian ports and ghost nets is built on the weakest segments in the research. And the realistic best case is probably a niche assurance service or a licence to an OEM or software vendor, since consolidation is the verified exit path and no pure-play civilian AI-sonar-review company has a disclosed revenue or price. If the eight-week tests find a named budget holder and a data-sharing pilot, the idea earns real investment. If they do not, the team has lost two months and gained a validated calibration method it can publish or fold into a research or grant project, which is a reasonable place to land.
