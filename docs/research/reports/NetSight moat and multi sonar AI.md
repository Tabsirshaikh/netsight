# Make the Trust Layer the Company

NetSight's non-market drawbacks can be fixed, but only if the team stops treating the detector as the product. Three things in NetSight carry across sonar types, resist copying and attract data, and they are one asset: the calibration and probability-of-detection (Pd) layer, a growing record of which contacts turned out to be real, and the team's independence from the vendors whose output it grades. On the technical side, a six-layer design holds up: format adapters, physics normalisation, a shared backbone with per-sensor heads, evidence fusion, per-sensor calibration, and a Pd model driven by sensor parameters. Every layer rests on established practice except the shared cross-sonar backbone. No one has published that, so pitch it as a research bet, not a capability. **Add multibeam backscatter as the second sensor.** It is usually recorded over the same seabed as side-scan, and NOAA publishes both for free in the same survey folder. The scarce data is not imagery. It is verified outcomes, and above all verified false alarms. NOAA's public-domain survey files and Descriptive Reports are the cheapest start. Offshore-wind UXO target lists are the richest source but need negotiated access. In India, the workable route is running the model on an institute's data rather than taking the data out. For defensibility, the ranking is: neutrality paid for by the risk owner first, then an open methodology and benchmark, then an outcome-label ledger secured by contract. Accreditation and an insurer-backed guarantee are year-two-plus goals, and patents come last. The company path is services-first. Start with a retrospective NOAA calibration study and a seeded-target trial that needs about **USD 5.4-6.7k for a sonar** (the boat is extra). Then sell fixed-price independent reviews, funded by Indian incubator grants of Rs 10-20 lakh before any defence bid. None of this overturns the earlier market finding that no buyer has yet been shown to pay for calibrated confidence. Every stage below therefore has a kill criterion. **The Stolte figures of EUR 140 per hectare and 5 diving days are still unverified and should stay out of every slide and document.**

*How to read the evidence labels.* "Verified" means a researcher opened the page and read the claim. "Search summary" means the claim rests on a search-engine snippet or a secondary summary and should be checked before it goes on a slide. "Opinion" marks our reasoning or design, not evidence. Market demand is not re-argued here. It appears only where it limits a choice; the full case is in the earlier reports `reports/Sonar survey market and buyers.md` and `reports/NetSight sonar problem validity.md`.

## Five of six layers rest on established practice; the shared backbone is the bet

Supporting many sonar types is mostly a normalisation and calibration problem, not a problem of building one giant model. The useful distinction is between the *form* of the trust layer and its *fitted values*. Calibration methods (isotonic regression, conformal prediction) act on scores and need only a held-out calibration set. Through-the-sensor Pd models of the kind NATO CMRE and Norway's FFI built for mine hunting act on geometry and seabed-complexity features. So the code is sensor-agnostic. The fitted parameters are not: conformal guarantees assume the calibration data look like the deployment data, so each sensor, and ideally each seabed type, needs its own fit (opinion, drawing on [Andéol et al. 2023](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v204/andeol23a/andeol23a.pdf), search summary). This is good news for a small team. The part NetSight claims as new is also the part that carries across sensors most cheaply.

| Layer | What it does | Best evidence | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| L0 Adapters | One reader per format, outputting a common record of samples, navigation, attitude, altitude and a sensor descriptor (frequency, beamwidths, range resolution) | Open readers exist for XTF, JSF, GSF, KMALL, s7k and Oculus ([MB-System](https://github.com/dwcaress/MB-System), [pyxtf](https://github.com/oysstu/pyxtf), [gsfu](https://github.com/valschmidt/gsfu), [oculus-python](https://pypi.org/project/oculus-python)) | Established; licences mostly unchecked |
| L1 Normalisation | Ground-range geometry, resolution tiers, gain and angle correction, per-pixel grazing-angle and range channels | GeoHab backscatter guidelines ([NIWA BSWG report](https://webstatic.niwa.co.nz/static/BWSG_REPORT_MAY2015_web.pdf), search summary) | Established for multibeam backscatter; joint four-sensor version is NetSight's own engineering |
| L2 Backbone + heads | Generic optical init, continued self-supervised training on all sonar tiles, small heads or adapters per sensor family | Single-modality results only ([Mine-JEPA](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.00383v1), [BenthicDINO](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.23215), verified) | **Unproven across modalities** |
| L3 Fusion | Associate detections across pings, passes and sensors in map coordinates | Bayesian residual-risk maps ([Dugelay et al., UACE 2019](https://www.uaconferences.org/docs/2019_papers/UACE2019_927_Dugelay.pdf), search summary) | Established in mine countermeasures |
| L4 Calibration | Isotonic or beta calibration plus group-wise conformal by sensor x frequency x seabed class, with a shift detector | Conformal detection literature ([arXiv 2403.07263](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2403.07263), search summary) | Established method; no sonar-specific paper found |
| L5 Pd / coverage | Pd per cell as a function of target size relative to resolution, grazing angle, range, seabed complexity, number of looks and sensor | FFI and CMRE through-the-sensor models ([ResearchGate, FFI](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/323254213_Through-the-sensor_performance_evaluation_for_modern_mine_hunting_operations), search summary) | Established in mine hunting |

The backbone evidence is narrower than it looks, so read it carefully. **Mine-JEPA**, pretrained on only 1,170 unlabelled side-scan images, beat a fine-tuned DINOv3 (a model pretrained on 1.7 billion images) on mine classification: F1 0.935 versus 0.922. But continuing self-supervised training *on top of* DINOv3 made it worse by 10.4 points, and the paper ran no cross-sensor tests ([Mine-JEPA](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.00383v1), verified). **BenthicDINO** is the only published evidence that geometry-aware pretraining transfers between side-scan systems. It was pretrained on Klein 3000H data at 500 kHz, with a penalty that pushes features to ignore incidence angle and range, and evaluated frozen on EdgeTech 2205 data at 520 kHz. It reached 0.423 shipwreck IoU, against 0.411 for a supervised U-Net. The same model scored 0.651 IoU on sites deeper than 40 m and 0.280 on shallow ones ([BenthicDINO](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.23215), verified). The frequencies are close and both sensors are side-scan, so this says nothing about side-scan to forward-looking sonar. The clearest measure of a domain gap is optical-to-side-scan detection. There, a source-only model reached mAP 9.58 against a supervised ceiling of 58.88, and combining style transfer with teacher-student adaptation recovered 48.64 ([Wei et al., Sci. Rep. 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11148073/), verified). Real side-scan data from two seas shows the same gap: GhostNetZero's Baltic-trained model fell from 0.740 to 0.547 mIoU on Puget Sound, and joint training recovered 0.739 and 0.685 ([GhostNetZero preprint](https://ecoevorxiv.org/repository/object/10294/download/19034), verified). Expect a real drop every time a sensor or site changes, measure it, and budget a few hundred target-sensor labels to recover it (opinion).

Normalisation is what makes the rest auditable. Convert slant range to ground range for side-scan and SAS. Project multibeam beams onto the seafloor. Convert forward-looking fans to Cartesian grids. Resample each sensor to a *resolution tier* (centimetres for SAS, about 5-20 cm for side-scan, decimetres to a metre for multibeam backscatter) instead of upsampling coarse data to fine. Then pass grazing angle, slant range, altitude, frequency and beamwidth as extra channels the model can condition on (opinion). Two findings support tiering: a SAR study found that feature-level adaptation is unreliable across resolutions ([arXiv 2507.08290](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2507.08290), search summary), and BenthicDINO showed that explicitly removing viewing geometry from features helps transfer. For multibeam backscatter, the GeoHab Backscatter Working Group guidelines (Lurton and Lamarche, 2015; journal summary 2018) are the community standard for angular-response and sector-pattern correction ([Lamarche and Lurton 2018](https://archimer.ifremer.fr/doc/00385/49655), search summary). Following them lets NetSight say "we normalise to the published standard" rather than "we tuned it". The exact correction formula in chapter 6.7 was not read and needs checking.

Calibration needs more data per sensor than the proposal assumes. NetSight's design document says a new sensor or site needs "~100 labelled contacts" for a refit. A MathWorks split-conformal workflow cites about **1,000 calibration samples per class** for stable quantiles ([MathWorks](https://www.mathworks.com/help/deeplearning/ug/quantify-uncertainty-in-object-detection-using-split-conformal-prediction.html), search summary). Our opinion: about 100 contacts can support a rough isotonic refit shown with wide confidence bands. It cannot support marketing a "calibrated" probability on that sensor. The product should therefore show a visible "calibration not yet validated for this sensor" state, and an out-of-distribution check on the normalised features should switch the output to "uncalibrated" whenever inputs drift. For Pd, fit one hierarchical model with sensor as a random effect, so a new sensor borrows strength from the others while its own seeded-target data accumulate (opinion, following the FFI and CMRE approach, whose coefficient forms were not read).

The format layer brings a licensing trap. MB-System is GPL v3 and reads Reson 7k, among many formats ([MB-System README](https://github.com/dwcaress/MB-System), verified). Call it as a separate command-line step rather than linking it, so the commercial code stays clean (opinion). Python readers exist for XTF ([oysstu/pyxtf](https://github.com/oysstu/pyxtf)), XTF plus EdgeTech JSF including SAS ([xtfjsf](https://github.com/jreGH/xtfjsf)), GSF with a KMALL converter ([gsfu](https://github.com/valschmidt/gsfu)), Kongsberg KMALL ([pykmall](https://pypi.org/project/pykmall/1.1.2/)) and Blueprint Oculus logs ([oculus-python](https://pypi.org/project/oculus-python), an experimental 0.0.2 alpha). All of these are search summaries, and **none of their licences was checked**. That includes pyxtf, which the proposal lists as MIT. No open readers were found for Teledyne BlueView, R2Sonic, Norbit, Kongsberg HISAS SAS or Coda Echoscope 3D formats; those likely need vendor SDKs. GeoTIFF mosaics and BAG files are the vendor-neutral fallback, at the cost of losing the geometry channels.

### Add multibeam backscatter second, forward-looking sonar third

| Order | Sensor | Why | Free data to start | Main risk |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 (now) | Side-scan | Existing design, most public data | NOAA mosaics, USGS raw XTF, SubPipe, Bath, AI4Shipwrecks | Mosaic-only inputs hide the gain history |
| **2** | **Multibeam backscatter + bathymetry** | Co-acquired on most wind, cable and NOAA surveys; contractors already cross-check side-scan against it; a GeoHab normalisation standard exists | **NOAA MBAB folders on the same seabed as the side-scan mosaic** | Coarser resolution; vendor-specific sector corrections |
| 3 | Forward-looking / imaging (Oculus, Gemini, ARIS, Omniscan FS) | Used at the verification step of search and recovery; cheap hardware | DFKI UXO (74k frames, BSD-3), UATD, Marine Debris FLS | Polar, short-range, temporal data; different physics from waterfalls |
| 4 | SAS | Highest resolution | SCTD 3.0 (10k snippets, licence unknown), SASSED (129) | Scarce, defence-linked, access from India uncertain |
| 5 | 3D sonar and water column | Niche | Almost none public | Needs a point-cloud or voxel model; formats proprietary |

The case for multibeam backscatter second is mostly practical. NOAA survey H13684's NCEI folder holds side-scan mosaic GeoTIFFs at 900 kHz and 1 m, a multibeam backscatter (MBAB) folder, bathymetry grids and the Descriptive Report, all released into the public domain under CC0 ([NCEI H13684 directory](https://data.ngdc.noaa.gov/platforms/ocean/nos/coast/H12001-H14000/H13684/); [data.gov record](https://catalog.data.gov/dataset/h13684-nos-hydrographic-survey-2023-04-29), both verified). That gives NetSight paired two-sensor data over the same contacts at no cost. It also gives a direct test: does fusing a second sensor make per-cell Pd and per-contact calibration measurably better? The offshore-wind argument (backscatter is co-acquired, and contractors use it to confirm side-scan contacts) comes from the company-path notes and is our opinion, supported by a UXO workflow paper which found that annotations still needed camera and magnetometer data to be ground-truthed ([ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/356038528_Workflow_towards_autonomous_and_semi-automized_UXO_Survey_and_Detection), search summary). Forward-looking sonar is the better second choice only if the team commits to search and recovery: a Cerulean Omniscan 450 FS retails for about USD 1,750-3,757 ([Geo-matching](https://geo-matching.com/products/omniscan-450-fs), search summary), and SonarSAM's Apache-2.0 code shows foundation models adapt to that modality ([GitHub](https://github.com/wangsssky/SonarSAM), search summary). For UXO work, magnetometer fusion matters more than any extra sonar, so it is a strong later candidate even though it is not acoustic (opinion).

### The honest technical risks

The biggest risk is that the shared backbone simply does not transfer across modalities. Every published self-supervised result we found stays inside one modality or one sensor family, and forward-looking and water-column data are physically far from side-scan waterfalls. The fallback is acceptable: separate small models per sensor family, all feeding the same normalisation, calibration and Pd layers. That is why the backbone must not be the pitch. The second risk is that many clients deliver only vendor-processed GeoTIFF mosaics with unknown gain. That breaks radiometric normalisation and removes the grazing-angle channel, so calibration on mosaic-only inputs will be worse and must be labelled as such (opinion). The third is silent miscalibration after a sensor or site change, which the shift detector exists to catch. The fourth is evaluation that averages away failures. BenthicDINO's 0.651 versus 0.280 split by depth shows why every reported number should be broken down by depth, seabed type and sensor. The last is data access. Public SAS data is scarce, an IEEE paper on mine-hunting performance parameters across several side-looking sonars and frequencies could not be read ([IEEE 7404535](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/7404535/)), and the cross-frequency SAS splits in SCTD 3.0 have no published numbers yet ([arXiv 2608.08106](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.08106), verified). Reporting the first numbers on those splits would itself build credibility.

## Verified false alarms are the scarce data, and NOAA publishes some free

"Ground truth" means three different things, and the team should never mix them in one claim. **Expert disposition** is a hydrographer's or archaeologist's judgement about a contact. **Physical verification** means a diver, ROV, clearance or retrieval established what the contact was. **Known placement** means the target was laid at a known position, so it is real by construction. Most public sonar labels are the first kind. SASSED's own authors warn that their SAS seabed labels "were not verified against the actual seafloor" ([Mendeley Data](https://data.mendeley.com/datasets/s5j5gzr2vc), search summary). Calibration also needs the half of the picture most sources drop: contacts that turned out to be nothing. No public dataset we found provides verified false positives. That gap is both the main data problem and the moat opportunity.

Public datasets are numerous but small, and few carry a confirmed commercial-friendly licence. A 2025 survey catalogues about 25 public sonar datasets, most between a few hundred and about 10,000 images ([Gomes et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.03353), search summary, table extraction garbled). The ones whose licence we read on the host page are below. Everything else needs a licence check or a permission email before commercial use.

|---|---|---|---|---|

| SubPipe ([Zenodo 10808161](https://zenodo.org/records/10808161)) | Dual-frequency side-scan | 10,030 images, 6,335 pipeline boxes | **CC BY 4.0** (the GitHub code is GPL-3.0) | Verified |

This corrects the proposal in two places. First, SubPipe's licence is now confirmed as CC BY 4.0. Second, "BenthiCat" as the proposal describes it (about a million tiles with raw XTF and labels) was not confirmed under that name. The confirmed open set is the unlabelled 434k-patch sediment release, and BenthicDINO's paper describes its pretraining data as a BenthiCat subset of about a million tiles ([BenthicDINO](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.23215), verified). Check which release carries labels and on what terms.

NOAA is the cheapest source of real contacts with dispositions. Descriptive Reports describe how contacts were selected and dismissed. Some include a "Sidescan Sonar Contact Listing" appendix, for example H12396 ([NCEI H12396 DR](https://data.ngdc.noaa.gov/platforms/ocean/nos/coast/H12001-H14000/H12396/DR/H12396_DR.pdf), search summary), and survey F00459 notes that "many contacts were dismissed" ([F00459 DR](https://data.ngdc.noaa.gov/platforms/ocean/nos/coast/F00001-F02000/F00459/DR/F00459.pdf), search summary). USGS has republished several NOAA surveys with raw Klein XTF and contact listings ([USGS OFR 2008-1174 contacts](https://pubs.usgs.gov/of/2008/1174/html/contacts.html), search summary), which gives NetSight raw pings rather than only mosaics. The NOAA Wrecks and Obstructions database holds about 13,000 wrecks and 6,000 obstructions with no access constraints, but **salvaged or disproved wrecks are removed from AWOIS**, which throws away exactly the negatives calibration needs ([NOAA InPort](https://fisheries.noaa.gov/inport/item/39988), verified listing). Negatives therefore have to be pulled out of Descriptive Reports and feature files by hand. There is precedent: hydrographers reviewed **24,726 automatic side-scan detections** from two NOAA-contracted survey sheets to build ground truth for a real-versus-false-alarm classifier, which reportedly reached about 93% Pd at about 6% false-alarm rate ([Hydro International](https://www.hydro-international.com/content/article/automated-side-scan-data-analysis), search summary; the page returned 403). One limit matters for claims. A NOAA contact list is mostly expert disposition, sometimes confirmed by multibeam or a dive. A study built on it can show calibration against expert judgement. It cannot show Pd against misses nobody found.

The real gold is in physically verified campaigns, and none of it is packaged as a dataset. Offshore-wind UXO campaigns produce sonar or magnetometer contacts, then ROV identification as UXO, debris, boulder or nothing. Archaeology reports on the Archaeology Data Service reproduce some of these target assessments, for example all 26 UXO targets at Galloper ([ADS Galloper](https://archaeologydataservice.ac.uk/catalogue/adsdata/arch-425-1/dissemination/pdf/1014026/wessexar1-518213_213954.pdf), search summary), and Scottish marine licence UXO appendices are public PDFs ([marine.gov.scot](https://marine.gov.scot/sites/default/files/technical_appendix_19.2_-_unexploded_ordnance_technical_report.pdf), search summary). The raw sonar behind them sits with developers and contractors. Ghost-gear removal programmes are the most mission-aligned source, because retrieval is verification. Virginia's programme recovered **9,970 derelict pots, 52 nets and 532 other items** using side-imaging sonar run by watermen ([VIMS](https://www.vims.edu/newsandevents/topstories/archives/2011/ghost_pots_2011.php), search summary). GhostNetZero's 239 Baltic and 173 Puget Sound annotated segments carry **no data-availability statement**, and their labels are expert annotations rather than diver confirmations ([GhostNetZero preprint](https://ecoevorxiv.org/repository/object/10294/download/19034), verified). The UK Marine Data Exchange says more than 60% of offshore-renewables survey data in England, Wales and Northern Ireland is freely available ([IMDIS 2018](https://imdis.seadatanet.org/content/download/122262/file/S1P032_IMDIS2018.pdf), search summary). But it requires login, its terms were not visible, and some Crown Estate data carry "internal business use" licences, which would block commercial training.

India has the sensors but no open channel. GSI's RV Samudra Ratnakar carries a SAMS DT-6000 synthetic aperture mapping sonar, and GSI bought a GeoAcoustics 2094D side-scan system in 2013 ([Kongsberg 2013](https://www.kongsberg.com/maritime/news-and-events/news-archive/2013/kongsberg-geoacoustics-sidescan-sonar-package-for-new-indian-survey-vessel); [Current Science note](https://i-scholar.in/index.php/CURS/article/view/156023/143870), search summary). Access runs through NDSAP, under which restricted data is available "only through authorization" against each agency's negative list ([NDSAP slides](https://de.slideshare.net/DataPortalIndia/national-data-sharing-and-accessibility-policy-ndsap-2012), search summary). INCOIS uses committee-approved and paid custom access ([CODATA DSJ 2018](https://datascience.codata.org/article/10.5334/dsj-2018-011), search summary). The 2021 geospatial guidelines kept limits on sensitive data ([TEAL white paper](https://tealindia.github.io/research/TEAL%20White%20Paper%208-%20Geospatial%20Regulations%20White%20Paper.pdf), search summary). No public Indian side-scan dataset turned up. The realistic route is an MoU with NIOT, NIO, GSI or IIT Madras under which the data stays on their premises and NetSight runs its model there. This "model-to-data" setup sidesteps both the sharing policy and geospatial export concerns (opinion). It yields local seabed priors and a validation sector, not a training corpus the team owns.

Synthetic data helps training but cannot certify anything. Adding diffusion-generated side-scan to real data reportedly lifted mine-like-object AP by about 60% over real-only ([Syn2Real, arXiv 2410.12953](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.12953v1), search summary, abstract level). Simulators such as HoloOcean now cover side-scan, multibeam and forward-looking sonar ([HoloOcean docs](https://byu-holoocean.github.io/holoocean-docs/v2.2.2/index.html)). The rule follows directly (opinion): use synthetic and injected targets for pretraining and for Pd sweeps where position is exact, but fit and report calibration only on real data with known outcomes.

## Neutrality is the one feature a sonar vendor cannot ship

Start with what cannot defend NetSight: detection accuracy, the calibration algorithm, a generic Pd map and a good interface. Vendors already give detection away inside their products. Coda Octopus claims 4,600 boulder contacts in 3 km of line data in 9 minutes, versus about three days by hand ([Ocean News](https://www.oceannews.com/k46h), vendor claim). Ocean Geophysics says its boulder AI "will not be sold as a stand-alone product" ([Offshore Engineer](https://www.oedigital.com/news/512069-ai-tool-to-tackle-boulder-issues-for-offshore-wind-developments), verified). Chesapeake says its coming detections will "carry probability estimates" ([Maritime Magazines](https://www.maritimemagazines.com/marine-technology/202602/the-future-of-sonar-processing)). SeeByte has already published a terrain-aware ATR performance map built by inserting simulated contacts into real side-scan ([arXiv 2404.18663](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.18663v1)), which is conceptually NetSight's Pd study. The drone-mapping analogy fits: DJI Terra processes only DJI data and undercuts Pix4D and DroneDeploy, whose defence is being drone-agnostic ([SkyeBrowse](https://www.skyebrowse.com/news/posts/dji-terra-vs-pix4d), comparison site, prices approximate).

What vendors cannot credibly offer is an independent grade of their own output. The credit-rating evidence is the sharpest example. After S&P switched in 1974 from investors paying for ratings to issuers paying, its ratings rose to match Moody's, which researchers attribute to the conflict of interest rather than to new information ([Harvard Law School Forum](https://corpgov.law.harvard.edu/2011/11/18/does-it-matter-who-pays-for-bond-ratings/), search summary). A counter-study finds no favouritism near rating thresholds once reputation and post-crisis regulation are taken into account ([PolyU](https://ira.lib.polyu.edu.hk/handle/10397/91324), search summary), so the effect is real but not automatic. In marine work, Skuld calls the warranty surveyor's independence "one of the most important factors in their engagement" ([Skuld](https://www.skuld.com/topics/ship/safety/the-marine-warranty-surveyor/), search summary), and marine warranty survey is usually a condition of an offshore-wind developer's Construction All Risk insurance ([ABL](https://abl-group.com/case-studies/gwynt-y-mor-marine-warranty-survey/), search summary). Independence also pays. Intertek, Bureau Veritas and SGS reported 2025 adjusted operating margins of **18.1%, 16.3% and 16.0%** at multi-billion revenue ([Bureau Veritas FY2025](https://group.bureauveritas.com/newsroom/sector-leading-organic-revenue-growth-65-fy-2025-strong-margin-improvement-163-fy), search summary). The same evidence carries two warnings. First, who pays decides whether you are neutral: if the survey contractor whose list you grade pays NetSight, NetSight inherits the issuer-pays problem. Second, those testing majors and the classification societies (DNV, Lloyd's Register, Bureau Veritas) are the natural incumbents for any "AI sonar assurance". A small team should aim to be the specialist method and tool *behind* them or behind a warranty surveyor, not their rival (opinion).

| Rank | Moat | Why a vendor struggles to copy it | Realism for a student team | First step | Evidence strength |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Neutrality paid for by the risk owner** | An OEM or contractor would be grading itself | High; costs little | Written impartiality policy modelled on ISO/IEC 17020; public pledge of no OEM or contractor equity or exclusivity; support at least three formats; sell to the developer, warranty surveyor, UXO consultant or port, not the contractor | Strong analogues, no sonar-specific case |
| 2 | **Open methodology and benchmark** | The author of the reference method owns the vocabulary | High; mostly writing and code | Preprint plus open benchmark (NOAA CC0 contacts, injected targets, scoring script), mapped to IHO S-44 and CIRIA C754 terms; invite vendors to be scored | DNV-RP-0671 precedent |
| 3 | **Outcome-label ledger** | Confirmed outcomes are scarce, and owners won't share them with conflicted parties | Medium; depends on contracts | Data-rights clause in every pilot for anonymised contact outcomes; a schema covering contact, outcome and conditions | a16z reasoning (opinion essay) |
| 4 | Tool behind accredited sign-off, then own accreditation | Certificates are what buyers already pay for | Medium-low before year 2 | Partner with one warranty surveyor, UXO consultancy or NABCB-accredited Indian inspection agency; keep 17020-style records from day one | Fee base only |
| 5 | Insurer-backed guarantee | Needs calibrated loss data only the ledger provides | Low before a track record | Offer contractual service credits first; approach HSB or a Lloyd's MGA later | One precedent, no claims data |
| 6 | Narrow technical patent | Core methods are already published | Low value | At most one Indian provisional on a specific technical pipeline; keep data and sensor models as trade secrets | Clear legal tests |

Publishing the method is a moat when the publisher is also the trusted reference. DNV's recommended practice "Assurance of AI-enabled systems" (DNV-RP-0671, September 2023) is free to download, frames assurance as claims backed by evidence, assesses the whole system rather than only the model, and calls for continuous validation instead of one-off "rubber stamps" ([DNV](https://www.dnv.com/digital-trust/recommended-practices/assurance-of-ai-enabled-systems-dnv-rp-0671/), search summary). IIHS shows the same pattern with an adversarial twist: carmakers dispute its methods yet redesign vehicles to score well ([Cars.com](https://www.cars.com/articles/automakers-respond-quickly-to-new-iihs-front-crash-prevention-test-505130), search summary). For NetSight, an open "sonar detection confidence and coverage assurance" method, structured like DNV-RP-0671, plus a benchmark on public NOAA contacts and the SCTD 3.0 cross-frequency splits, does three jobs (opinion). It invites scrutiny, which builds credibility. It makes the team the reference implementation. And it puts the method on the public record before anyone else patents it. Keep the outcome ledger, the per-sensor injection models and the composition of the calibration set private.

The data moat is real only for outcome labels. a16z's essay argues that most "data moats" are scale effects with diminishing returns, that the moat "erodes as the data corpus grows", and that data defends best when sources are scarce, reluctant to share with several vendors, and trust a responsible custodian ([a16z](https://a16z.com/the-empty-promise-of-data-moats/), verified opinion essay). Sonar imagery fails that test, since vendors and contractors hold far more of it. Confirmed outcomes pass it, and every confirmed outcome directly improves NetSight's core claim. The ledger only grows if contracts grant access to anonymised post-investigation results, and returns will flatten quickly on common targets, so the value concentrates in rare seabed, burial and sensor combinations (opinion).

Accreditation is a slow barrier, not a launch feature. In India, ISO/IEC 17020 inspection bodies are accredited by NABCB and ISO/IEC 17025 labs by NABL. NABCB's procedure runs through application, document review, on-site and witness assessment, and its fees follow a rate of **US$800 per assessor man-day plus travel**, non-refundable ([NABCB BCB 201 IB, Feb 2026](https://nabcb.qci.org.in/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/BCB-201-IB-Accreditation-Procedure-for-Inspection-Bodies_Feb-2026.pdf); [fee structure](https://nabcb.qci.org.in/wp-content/uploads/2023/08/BCB%20F002%20-%20NABCB%20Fee%20structure%20IBs%20-%20Mar%202023.pdf), search summary). Our extrapolation, not a quote: 5-10 assessment man-days would be about US$4-8k in assessment fees alone, before application and annual fees and the internal quality-system effort. The timeline annex could not be read. ISO/IEC 17020 fits better than 17025 because it covers judgements of conformity ("this survey meets coverage requirement X"). No source shows any accreditation body accrediting AI-based inspection judgements yet.

The insurer-backed guarantee is the strongest moat and the latest one. Augury launched "Guaranteed Diagnostics" in 2020, a warranty of up to $100,000 if its machine-health AI misses a failure, backed by Munich Re's HSB. It added a production-loss guarantee in 2023, and HSB said it planned similar cover for other AI firms ([TechTarget](https://www.techtarget.com/searchenterpriseai/news/252484554/Predictive-maintenance-vendor-launches-AI-warranty); [BusinessWire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20231205450358/en), search summary). No claims or uptake data was found, and one analyst warned such policies can do "as much harm as good". A NetSight guarantee ("we pay if an object above size X is later found in a zone we rated at least Y% searched") needs exactly the calibrated loss history the ledger builds. So it belongs in year two or later, with service credits as the stand-in until then (opinion).

Patents are the weakest option for this team. India's Section 3(k) excludes mathematical methods, algorithms and computer programs per se. The 2025 Computer-Related Inventions Guidelines (finalised around July 2025) focus on technical effect and say AI used as one step in a larger technical solution is not excluded ([EU IP Helpdesk](https://intellectual-property-helpdesk.ec.europa.eu/news-events/news/indian-patent-office-guidance-examining-ai-inventions-cri-guidelines-2025-2025-08-05_en), search summary). The USPTO's July 2024 guidance keeps the Alice/Mayo test, under which bare mathematical manipulation is abstract but integration into a practical application can be eligible ([USPTO](https://www.uspto.gov/about-us/news-updates/uspto-issues-ai-subject-matter-eligibility-guidance), search summary). "Calibrated probability of a contact" is unpatentable and is already prior art. SeeByte's target insertion is published, so even injection-based Pd mapping needs a freedom-to-operate check. A possible claim would be a specific technical pipeline in which sensor-modelled injection drives a georeferenced coverage map that controls re-survey line planning. It is useful as a fundraising signal; enforcing it against Kongsberg or Fugro is not realistic (opinion).

Neutrality breaks easily, and the team should decide now what it will refuse. Strategic money or an exclusive deal from a sonar OEM or a large survey contractor would undo moat number one (opinion). The company notes flag Planys, the IIT Madras ROV-inspection spin-out, as a natural partner or first customer, and also as a possible competitor or acquirer. Its investors have included the ONGC Startup Fund and Shell ([YourStory 2021](https://yourstory.com/2021/11/marine-robotics--planys-technologies-iit-madras), search summary). Work with such firms as data and delivery partners under non-exclusive terms, and keep the assurance brand separate from any one of them.

## Prove value on public data, then sell reviews, not software

### Two cheap proofs answer different questions

The retrospective NOAA study proves calibration. The seeded-target trial proves Pd. Do not let either stand in for the other. The retrospective study takes 20-40 NOAA H-surveys that have Descriptive Report contact listings, runs NetSight, and scores its probabilities against hydrographer dispositions with reliability diagrams, expected calibration error and Brier scores. It costs mainly student time, because the data is CC0. It shows whether "80%" means 80% against expert judgement. It cannot show how many real objects were missed, because nobody knows the misses (opinion, from the company-path notes). Pd needs targets of known size and position. Lay them in a lake, harbour or university basin, survey at several ranges, speeds and seabed types, and compare predicted with observed detection. A Cerulean Omniscan 450 side-scan costs about **USD 5,440 on the Blue Robotics BlueBoat page, GBP 5,640 at RobotShop UK and USD 6,700 at another reseller**, plus an integration kit of about GBP 250 ([RobotShop UK](https://uk.robotshop.com/products/cerulean-omniscan-450-side-scan-sonar-for-marine-exploration-blueboat-bluerov2); [Charpierov](https://charpierov.com/?p=9549), search summary). The boat price was not captured, and no Indian hire rates for USVs, ROVs or Sonobots were found, so borrowing a vessel from NIOT, IIT Madras or Planys may be cheaper than buying. Both studies mirror published practice. The University of Liverpool boulder thesis criticises earlier UXO classifiers for publishing only training accuracy with no validation ([Liverpool](https://livrepository.liverpool.ac.uk/3194759/1/201666320_Aug2025.pdf), search summary). A team that publishes held-out reliability diagrams and a measured Pd curve is already ahead of that bar.

Claims must be worded exactly. **The EUR 140 per hectare diver-search cost and the "5 diving days for 49 positions" figure attributed to Stolte et al. 2022 are unverified.** Repeated searches failed to find them, and the fetched text of the paper did not contain them. They should be removed from the proposal's "Dives are expensive" row. What Stolte does support is 549 suspect positions over 5,820 ha in 45 days, 176 checked by divers and 94 confirmed as lost gear, with no miss rate reported ([Frontiers](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fmars.2022.981840)). The accurate line is "about half of the suspects divers checked were real gear", not "experts are right only half the time".

### Services-first has the best evidence in this industry

Ocean Geophysics, a UK marine-geophysics processor, built an ML boulder detector and folds it into its "specialist processing, interpreting, and reporting" service instead of selling it ([Offshore Engineer](https://www.oedigital.com/news/512069-ai-tool-to-tackle-boulder-issues-for-offshore-wind-developments), verified). Cyberhawk called itself "an inspection company first and foremost" for six years before its iHawk software became a major part of the business ([DataRoot Labs](https://datarootlabs.com/blog/chris-fleming-cyberhawk-interview), search summary). Planys raised staged rounds from an angel round of about USD 260k in 2015 to a reported Rs 100 crore while selling inspection services ([iTWire](https://itwire.com/it-industry-news/strategy/planys-raises-%E2%82%B9100-crore-to-accelerate-global-expansion-and-launch-defence-arm,-planys-ark), search summary). The product-first counter-example, Andrenam's reported USD 10 m seed and USD 18 m Series A for sonar-analysis AI, is defence-funded and its details are unconfirmed ([Dealroom](https://dealroom.co/news/140710-underwater-sensing-startup-andrenam-lands-18m-series-a-to-scale-pearl-bu/), search summary). For NetSight this means a services-first "assurance desk" (opinion). Sell a fixed-price independent second opinion on a contractor's target list and coverage, delivered as a signed report and paid for by the risk owner. Run the tool internally. Productise into an API or plug-in only after about 10-20 paid jobs have produced labelled outcomes. Each job feeds the ledger, which answers the data and copying drawbacks together. The earlier market research adds one integration constraint. HYPACK's SDK is gated, needs a separate agreement for bundling, and forbids using SDK materials "to train or fine-tune AI or machine learning systems" ([Xylem HYPACK](https://www.xylem.com/en-qa/brand/hypack/integrate-with-hypack/)). A file-in, report-out workflow that reads exported XTF, JSF or GeoTIFF is the clean path.

### Partnerships: copy the GhostNetBusters structure, avoid exclusivity

The best documented template is GhostNetBusters (June 2024 to January 2026). It joined GEOMAR as research lead, sonoware GmbH as the sonar-software SME, and One Earth - One Ocean e.V. for net recovery and dive verification. It was funded with **EUR 221k** from Schleswig-Holstein's AI fund and released an open-source tool ([sonoware](https://sonoware.de/news/2024-08-28_foerderzusage_ghostnetbusters); [GEOMAR](https://www.geomar.de/en/ghostnetbusters), search summary). An Indian version would pair an institute (NIOT, NIO or IIT Madras), NetSight, and an operator whose dives or recoveries provide verification: an NGO, a port, or Planys. The hosts with concrete maritime programmes are NTCPWC at IIT Madras (set up in 2018 under the Sagarmala programme, about Rs 77 crore per a Lok Sabha answer) and the Cochin Shipyard and IIT Madras marine-startup programme (a reported Rs 50 crore corpus) ([Lok Sabha AU1271](https://eparlib.nic.in/bitstream/123456789/2973827/1/AU1271.pdf); [YourStory 2026](https://yourstory.com/2026/04/bharat-innovates-2026-iit-madras-india-blue-economy-france), search summary). No public contract terms for sonar data-sharing were found. Sensible shapes (opinion) are: data-for-validation NDAs with contractors, in which they get a free retrospective assurance report and NetSight gets anonymised outcomes; non-exclusive plug-in or co-marketing with OEMs such as Cerulean or Deep Trekker; and joint grant bids with universities under the incubator's IP policy.

### Legal, export and liability: say "evidence", never "clear"

Export control is probably not a blocker for a shallow-water civil tool, but it has to be checked, not assumed. Under the US EAR, side-scan and SAS seabed-imaging systems fall in ECCN 6A001.a.1 only if they meet technical thresholds, one of which is design for depths beyond 500 m. The sources found are archived 2001-2013 texts, so the current wording may differ ([archived BIS CCL Cat 6](https://webarchive.library.unt.edu/web/20130216093249/http:/www.bis.doc.gov/policiesandregulations/ear/ccl6.pdf), search summary). India's SCOMET list controls dual-use items, software and technology, with authorisations from the DGFT SCOMET Cell. The exact sonar entry was not confirmed ([DGFT FTP Chapter 10](https://content.dgft.gov.in/Website/HBP_07_2026_Chapter_10.pdf), search summary). Get a formal classification before the first foreign contract (opinion). On liability, offshore UXO work runs on CIRIA C754 (2015): mitigate risk to "as low as reasonably practicable" (ALARP), issue a sign-off certificate, and send non-intrusive survey targets on to intrusive investigation ([CIRIA](https://www.ciria.org/CIRIA/Research/Projects_underway2/UXO_offshore_developments.aspx), search summary). Data licences have their own traps. NOAA data is marked "not to be used for navigation", MB-System is GPL, the proposal's Ultralytics YOLO is AGPL (YOLOX is the Apache-2.0 path the proposal already names), and the HYPACK SDK bans AI training on its materials. No evidence was found on professional-indemnity cover or premiums for survey QA, though specialist hydrographic-surveyor cover exists ([ITIC](https://www.itic-insure.com/fileadmin/uploads/itic/Documents/Factsheets/2026/Hydrographic_surveyors_fact_sheet_2026.pdf)).

| Do not say | Say instead |
|---|---|
| "The channel is clear" or "the area is safe" | "Cells shown green had an estimated Pd of at least 0.8 for a [stated reference target], with a 95% interval of [a-b]" |
| "Certified" (before accreditation) | "Independent probabilistic second-opinion assessment of the supplied data" |
| "Calibrated" on a sensor without validation data | "Calibration not yet validated for this sensor; scores are relative" |
| "Works on any sonar" | "Reads XTF, JSF, GSF and KMALL; validated so far on [listed sensors]" |
| "Experts are right only half the time" | "About half of the suspects divers checked were real gear (94 of 176; Stolte et al. 2022)" |
| "Dive search costs EUR 140/ha" | Do not use; the figure is unverified |
| "Replaces divers or analysts" | "Helps teams decide which contacts to investigate first and where to re-survey" |

A standard disclaimer (opinion): "NetSight provides a probabilistic second-opinion assessment of the supplied data. It does not certify the absence of objects. Residual risk remains and must be managed by the duty holder under the project's ALARP process."

## An 18-24 month roadmap where each stage can kill the company

The market constraint sets the shape. The earlier buyer research found no organisation shown to pay for calibrated confidence or coverage. India offers grants, data partners and cheap building, but not the first paying customer. The best-evidenced niche is independent assurance of offshore-wind and cable target lists, which is cyclical and only partly sonar-based. So the roadmap runs that research's eight-week interview test in parallel with the cheapest technical proof, and lets either one stop the project. Milestones and kill criteria are our judgement, built on the cited anchors. The only sourced cash costs are the sonar hardware and the NABCB man-day rate.

| Stage | Months | Goal | Milestones | Known cash cost | Kill criteria |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | 0-2 | Find the payer, secure data rights | 20-25 interviews (half outside India; warranty surveyors and UXO consultants first); licence check on every dataset; impartiality policy drafted | Near zero | No named budget holder **and** no organisation willing to share anonymised data (the earlier report's test) |
| 1 | 1-5 | Retrospective calibration | Parse 20-40 NOAA H-surveys plus USGS raw-XTF surveys into a contact-outcome table; reliability diagrams, expected calibration error and Brier score against a raw-score baseline; preprint plus open benchmark v0; incubator entry; apply for SISFS and NIDHI-PRAYAS | Compute and student time | Fewer than 10 surveys with usable contact listings, or calibration no better than the raw-score baseline at matched detection rate (the reported ~93% Pd / ~6% false-alarm SAIC result is a rough reference only) |
| 2 | 4-9 | Seeded-target Pd trial | Lay targets of known size at known positions; survey at several ranges, speeds and seabed types; Pd-vs-range curve and a predicted-vs-observed coverage map; injected-target Pd compared with real-target Pd | ~USD 5.4-6.7k sonar + ~GBP 250 kit, or a borrowed vessel | Predicted Pd misses observed detection beyond agreed confidence bounds, or no vessel and no grant by month 9 |
| 3 | 6-15 | Paid assurance pilots | 2-3 fixed-price independent target-list and coverage reviews (one EU/UK wind or cable consultant, one Indian port or dredging client, one contractor via the risk owner); data-rights clauses; 17020-style records | Travel and time | Fewer than 2 paying pilots by month 15, or clients refuse to return outcome data (no ledger, no moat) |
| 4 | 12-20 | Second sensor and firewall | Fuse NOAA MBAB backscatter with side-scan; publish the cross-sensor drop and the fusion gain; optionally report SCTD 3.0 cross-frequency numbers; export classification; separate defence workstream if an iDEX statement fits | Grant-dependent | Fusion gives no measurable calibration or Pd gain, or the export classification blocks civil exports |
| 5 | 18-24 | Productise and harden | API or plug-in after at least 10 labelled jobs; partnership with an accredited inspection agency or warranty surveyor; PI insurance; seed round or iDEX Prime at TRL 4+; first insurer conversation | Accreditation ~US$800/man-day if pursued | No repeat customer, or an incumbent bundles equivalent validated calibration for free |

The stages overlap on purpose: a student team can run interviews, NOAA parsing and grant applications in the same months. Two numbers deserve close tracking throughout. The first is the calibration-sample count per sensor, because it decides when a "validated" label is honest. The second is the count of physically verified outcomes in the ledger, because it decides when a guarantee becomes possible. Never plan around the gap between injected-target Pd and real-target Pd; measure it, because the proposal's own risk section names it as the central technical risk.

## Conclusion

The four drawbacks turn out to be one problem seen from four sides. Supporting many sonar types matters less as a feature than as a precondition for neutrality: an assessor that reads only one vendor's files cannot claim to be independent of that vendor. Neutrality, in turn, is what persuades contractors and developers to hand over the outcome labels that make calibration honest and harder to copy. Those outcomes are also the only route to the most valuable moat, a guarantee backed by someone else's balance sheet. That changes what the team should optimise. The detector can stay ordinary. The normalisation, calibration and Pd layers, the impartiality policy and the data-rights clause in every contract are where the company is built.

The binding unknown is not technical. Nothing in the research shows that a warranty surveyor, UXO consultant, insurer or port will accept AI-derived confidence and coverage evidence, and that party, not the survey contractor, has to be the payer for the neutrality moat to work. The first interviews should therefore go to the people who sign ALARP and warranty certificates, not to the people who pick targets. If they say no, the team still keeps an open, published calibration method and a benchmark on public NOAA data. That is a credible research contribution and a reasonable place to stop.
