## 1. How large are the marine/hydrographic/geophysical survey services market and the sonar equipment market, and how fast are they growing?

### Takeaway
No reliable, independently sourced figure exists for "sonar survey services." Market-research firms disagree by 1-2 orders of magnitude depending on scope: hydrographic survey equipment is roughly USD 2.5-4.1 bn (2025) at 5-7% CAGR, and side-scan sonar alone ranges from about USD 44-50 m to USD 275 m. The best real-money anchor is Fugro, the largest listed contractor, whose Marine division had about EUR 1.75 bn revenue in 2024 and fell about 15% in 2025 because of the offshore wind slowdown.

### Inferences
- The equipment-market consensus (about USD 3-4 bn, 5-7% CAGR) is the only cluster of agreeing figures, but it is made of low-reliability firms that probably copy each other's method. It covers all hydrographic gear (echosounders, positioning, USVs), not sonar alone.
- Side-scan is a small hardware niche (tens to a few hundred million USD). The money is in the services, so a software-only product is a thin slice of a mid-size market.
- Offshore wind is cyclical and was the swing factor: Fugro's Marine revenue dropped about 15% in a year because of it. A student team should not assume survey demand grows steadily.

### Gaps
- No primary-source (IHO, trade body, company filing text) figure for global survey services spend. Fugro's annual report text was not read directly; the 2025 Marine revenue figure for the full year is not confirmed (only the -15.5% change and the 9-month number).
- Multibeam, synthetic aperture sonar, and sonar-processing-software market sizes: not found.
- Gardline, TGS and Oceaneering survey revenue: not searched in this pass.

## 2. Which segments buy sonar surveys: typical spend, buyers, and share of cost that is interpretation labour?

### Takeaway
Hard segment-level spend figures were mostly not found. What is available: offshore wind survey is a small share of project cost (1-4% of total cost per Fugro, under 1% of CAPEX per Acteon) but huge in absolute terms; UXO target investigation is a major cost driver because only about 4% of investigated targets turn out to be UXO (vendor figure); search-and-recovery is lumpy, success-fee work (MH370: USD 70 m on find only). No source gave interpretation labour as a percentage of survey cost.

### Cited Findings
Offshore wind (site surveys, boulders, UXO, debris):
- Fugro slide deck (Nova Scotia offshore wind R&D forum, Sept 2025) puts geodata at 1-4% of total project cost, citing GWEC, BNEF and IRENA [VENDOR] — [Fugro deck](https://netzeroatlantic.ca/sites/default/files/2025-10/KSmith_Fugro_NovaScotia_RD_Forum_09242025.pdf)
- Acteon says site investigation is under 1% of CAPEX, and cites research of about GBP 4,700 per MW for floating-wind geotechnical survey (geotechnical, not geophysical or sonar). A specialist geotechnical vessel was described to a UK inquiry as about GBP 100,000/day (geotechnical vessel, not a sonar survey vessel) — [Guide to Floating Offshore Wind, geotechnical surveys](https://guidetofloatingoffshorewind.com/guide/p-development-and-project-management/p-4-geological-and-hydrographical-surveys/p-4-2-geotechnical-surveys/) [SEARCH-SUMMARY]
- Denmark's 2014 nearshore 350 MW tender survey projections ran about DKK 23-26 m per site (whole preliminary investigation package, 2014 prices, dated) — [offshoreWIND.biz](https://www.offshorewind.biz/?p=119651) [SEARCH-SUMMARY]
- Carbon Trust / Offshore Wind Industry Council guidance on UXO and boulder geophysical surveys: surveys in planning stages "can cost millions of pounds"; the guidance concluded the problem was survey design, not equipment. This implies repeat surveys and delays are the cost risk — [Carbon Trust](https://www.carbontrust.com/news-and-insights/news/new-guidance-for-offshore-wind-industry-on-geophysical-surveys-for-unexploded-ordnance-and-boulders), [WindTech](https://www.windtech-international.com/industry-news/new-guidance-for-offshore-wind-industry-on-geophysical-surveys-for-unexploded-ordnance-and-boulders) [SEARCH-SUMMARY]
- Demand trend: Fugro says offshore wind slowed in several countries and early-stage site characterisation fell; a meaningful rebound "would take time" (early 2026 reporting) — [Baird Maritime](https://www.bairdmaritime.com/amp/story/offshore/fugros-2025-profits-impacted-by-offshore-wind-slowdown) [SEARCH-SUMMARY]

UXO / munitions (offshore wind, cables, Baltic and North Sea):
- ALM Geophysics (Seequent article) [VENDOR]: vessel cost above GBP 50,000/day, about 10 targets investigated per day, and across 18 UXO campaign projects only about 4% of investigated targets were UXO. Cutting targets by 10% saves a meaningful number of vessel days — [Seequent / ALM](https://www.seequent.com/optimizing-uxo-target-lists-with-alm-geophysics-library-of-previously-found-targets/) [SEARCH-SUMMARY]
- GEOMAR / BASTA paper (peer-style conference paper, EU-funded) [PRIMARY-READ]: "During UXO survey and clearance campaigns, a large number of false positive target points drive costs and efforts"; "the majority of objects that are detected ... and later on investigated turn out to be non-UXO objects (i.e. false positives), such as anthropogenic scrap and natural objects such as boulders." A neural network on multibeam data "has the potential to significantly reduce the time it takes to manually pick target points," but in their test several ANN-flagged objects were probably not UXO ("additional training is required"). They also propose data quality factors to confirm survey data is adequate to detect a reference object before interpretation. No cost figures given — [GEOMAR PDF](https://oceanrep.geomar.de/id/eprint/55572/1/TacklingCostDriversofMaritimeEXOSurveys.pdf)
- Offshore Engineer (Jan 2026): magnetometry campaigns produce "thousands of red dots"; investigating target lists needs EOD experts and divers. Baltic and North Sea hold about 1.9 million tons of UXO. No cost or time figures in the article — [Offshore Engineer](https://www.maritimemagazines.com/offshore-engineer/202601/hidden-hazard-below) (fetched, read via summariser)

Subsea cables and pipelines:
- TeleGeography: new submarine cable construction spend averaged over USD 2 bn per year over the past nine years; cables planned for service 2025-2027 total over USD 13 bn (not all will be built). Survey cost share not given — [TeleGeography blog](https://blog.telegeography.com/building-tomorrows-internet-an-update-on-new-cable-investment) [SEARCH-SUMMARY]
- SubTel Forum applies a rule-of-thumb USD 25,000 per km of cable for unannounced costs (date unclear; this is total cable cost, not survey) — [SubTel Forum almanac](https://issuu.com/subtelforum/docs/almanac_issue_47) [SEARCH-SUMMARY]
- Hydro International describes route-survey scope (nearshore, shallow under 1,500 m, deep over 1,500 m, desktop study first), no costs — [Hydro International](https://www.hydro-international.com/content/article/subsea-cable-route-surveying)

Defence mine countermeasures (MCM):
- US Navy: Textron MCM USV software/payload integration/sustainment contract of about USD 100.1 m (probably a ceiling; about USD 4.7 m obligated at award), Feb 2025. Raytheon minehunt payload delivery system USD 18.3 m (AN/AQS-20, not SAS). Bollinger MCM USV advance order USD 7.7 m. Singapore MINDEF signed an unmanned MCM contract with ST Engineering in May 2025, including towed synthetic aperture sonars (value not given) — [Orange Slices contract summary](https://orangeslices.ai/?p=125378), [Naval News](https://www.navalnews.com/naval-news/2025/02/u-s-navy-awards-contracts-for-deployment-of-future-mcm-capabilities/), [MINDEF](https://www.mindef.gov.sg/news-and-events/latest-releases/7may25_nr/) [SEARCH-SUMMARY]
- Purpose of ATR in MCM: ease sonar operator workload and shorten post-mission analysis (PMA), with the operator keeping the final classification decision. A US Navy/DTIC presentation lists eliminating lengthy PMA as cutting the MCM timeline by up to 50%. Thales claims MiMap is "up to 4x faster" with "+99% correct classification" [VENDOR]. SeeByte ATR is being integrated on a towed SAS for the Danish and Polish navies — [DTIC](https://apps.dtic.mil/sti/pdfs/ADA576774.pdf), [Thales](https://www.thalesgroup.com/en/solutions-catalogue/defence/naval/advanced-sonar-data-analysis), [SeeByte](https://www.unmannedsystemstechnology.com/2021/05/automatic-target-recognition-integrated-onto-towed-sonar/) [SEARCH-SUMMARY]

Search-and-recovery:
- Malaysia / Ocean Infinity MH370 "no find, no fee" deal: USD 70 m only if wreckage is found. The 2025-26 search covered new areas of the southern Indian Ocean (reported 7,571 sq km searched in one report; 15,000 sq km planned area in another), ended without findings on 23 January 2026, so no fee was paid. Contract reportedly ran to June 2026 — [Australian Aviation](https://australianaviation.com.au/2026/03/no-luck-for-ocean-infinity-in-latest-hunt-for-mh370/), [AvWeb](https://avweb.com/aviation-news/renewed-mh370-search-concludes-no-findings/), [SCMP](https://www.scmp.com/news/asia/southeast-asia/article/3303034/malaysia-agrees-no-find-no-fee-terms-restart-flight-mh370-search) [SEARCH-SUMMARY]

### Inferences
- The strongest "review is the cost" evidence is in UXO: only about 4% of investigated targets are UXO, investigation runs at about 10 targets/day at GBP 50k+/day, so every false target avoided has a direct vessel-day price. This is a vendor figure (ALM sells target-list software), so it needs independent confirmation.
- Defence MCM money is large, but the buyer wants certified/ATR-integrated systems from primes (Thales, Raytheon, SeeByte, Textron). A student startup is unlikely to win that directly.
- Search-and-recovery is real but episodic, and a "calibrated confidence plus coverage map" has natural value there (proving an area was searched), but the budget per event is hard to predict.

### Gaps
- Typical spend per project in port/channel dredging, oil and gas, archaeology, ghost gear/marine debris, and insurance/salvage: not found. I did not search these segments specifically.
- Share of survey cost that is interpretation/processing labour: not found for any segment.
- Per-km2 or per-line-km price for offshore wind UXO/boulder surveys: not found (a search specifically returned no cost-per-km2 figure).
- 6 Alpha, Ordtek, CIRIA guidance: not retrieved.

## 3. What do contractors say about the processing/interpretation bottleneck, and are there cost-per-line-km or vessel-day figures?

### Takeaway
Qualitative evidence for the bottleneck is solid (skills shortage, data volumes, false-positive target lists), but I found no published figure for hours of manual review per line-km, backlog length, or cost per line-km. The only hard cost anchors are vessel-day rates (GBP 50k+/day sonar/UXO vessel from a vendor; about GBP 100k/day specialist geotechnical vessel).

### Cited Findings
- Hydro International 2025 industry survey: availability and development of skilled professionals is the most frequently mentioned theme, tied to an ageing workforce, limited inflow of new hydrographers and rising workloads. Consultants see automation/AI as an immediate answer; hydrographers appear not to, and expect autonomous platforms, automated processing and AI tools to work alongside, not replace, skilled people. One respondent: AI will speed up processing "but you still need experienced and knowledgeable personnel to evaluate the results" — [Hydro International, The pressure beneath the surface](https://www.hydro-international.com/content/article/the-pressure-beneath-the-surface) (page returned 403 when fetched; content via search summary) [SEARCH-SUMMARY]
- A 2022 Hydro International survey of offshore survey firms: "a striking number" of participants highlighted difficulty finding personnel with data acquisition and processing knowledge — [Hydro International 5-2022](https://issuu.com/geomarespublishing/docs/hydro_5-2022_bg_lr-pdf/s/17658851) [SEARCH-SUMMARY]
- Other Hydro International pieces on talent and skills: [Search for talent is hydrographic industry's biggest challenge](https://www.hydro-international.com/content/article/search-for-talent-is-hydrographic-industry-s-biggest-challenge), [Skills and tech: hydrography's balancing act](https://www.hydro-international.com/content/article/skills-and-tech-hydrography-s-balancing-act) (titles seen only)
- FIG 2025 presentation lists data cleaning and filtering and data quality monitoring as key automation areas, citing time savings and accuracy (no numbers in summary) — [FIG 2025 TS07G](https://fig.net/resources/proceedings/fig_proceedings/fig2025/ppt/ts07g/TS07G_aik_moh_davies_13489_ppt.pdf) [SEARCH-SUMMARY]
- Bibby HydroMap / Seequent case study: needed something faster than spreadsheets to process and interpret marine UXO data (no time figures in summary) [VENDOR] — [Seequent case study](https://www.casestudies.com/company/seequent/case-study/bibby-hydromap-customer-case-study) [SEARCH-SUMMARY]
- GEOMAR/BASTA: manual target picking is time-consuming; ANN can generate a target list "instantly" but needs complete, accurate annotated training data, and its output still needed validation by magnetic AUV survey [PRIMARY-READ] — [GEOMAR PDF](https://oceanrep.geomar.de/id/eprint/55572/1/TacklingCostDriversofMaritimeEXOSurveys.pdf)
- Vessel-day anchors: above GBP 50,000/day with about 10 targets/day [VENDOR] — [Seequent / ALM](https://www.seequent.com/optimizing-uxo-target-lists-with-alm-geophysics-library-of-previously-found-targets/); about GBP 100,000/day specialist geotechnical vessel (UK inquiry evidence, geotechnical not sonar) — [Guide to Floating Offshore Wind](https://guidetofloatingoffshorewind.com/guide/p-development-and-project-management/p-4-geological-and-hydrographical-surveys/p-4-2-geotechnical-surveys/) [SEARCH-SUMMARY]

### Inferences
- Interpreters are scarce, and hydrographers themselves doubt AI will replace them. A product that positions as "assists the reviewer, shows calibrated confidence, and documents coverage" fits that stance better than "replaces the reviewer."
- Review automation pays most where it reduces vessel-days (UXO target reduction), not where it only saves desk hours. The wedge is shrinking target lists with calibrated probabilities.
- A calibrated confidence score is exactly what the GEOMAR work shows is missing: their ANN output was uncertain and needed AUV validation.

### Gaps
- Hours of manual review per line-km, backlog statistics, and cost per line-km: not found in any source. Needs interviews with survey processors or contractor tender rate cards.
- Post-processing share of project cost: not found.
- No independent evaluation of ATR time savings; all quantified gains are vendor or programme claims.

## 5. Which segment looks most promising for an AI review/confidence layer, and which least?

### Takeaway
Based on the evidence available (thin and partly vendor-sourced), offshore wind and cable UXO/boulder/debris target screening looks the most promising: recurring campaigns, regulatory insistence on risk-based proof, a documented false-positive cost, and a direct vessel-day price per wasted investigation. Least promising for a student startup: government/defence MCM (certification, primes, long procurement) and one-off search-and-recovery (lumpy, success-fee budgets). Caveat: offshore wind demand is currently cyclical-down.

### Cited Findings
- All findings are in sections 1-4. Key supports: Fugro Marine revenue -15.5% in 2025 on offshore wind slowdown, [Baird Maritime](https://www.bairdmaritime.com/amp/story/offshore/fugros-2025-profits-impacted-by-offshore-wind-slowdown); UXO false-positive cost, [GEOMAR PDF](https://oceanrep.geomar.de/id/eprint/55572/1/TacklingCostDriversofMaritimeEXOSurveys.pdf) and [Seequent / ALM](https://www.seequent.com/optimizing-uxo-target-lists-with-alm-geophysics-library-of-previously-found-targets/); hydrographer skepticism of full automation, [Hydro International](https://www.hydro-international.com/content/article/the-pressure-beneath-the-surface); MCM ATR already served by established vendors, [Thales](https://www.thalesgroup.com/en/solutions-catalogue/defence/naval/advanced-sonar-data-analysis) and [SeeByte](https://www.unmannedsystemstechnology.com/2021/05/automatic-target-recognition-integrated-onto-towed-sonar/); MH370 success-fee structure, [SCMP](https://www.scmp.com/news/asia/southeast-asia/article/3303034/malaysia-agrees-no-find-no-fee-terms-restart-flight-mh370-search)

### Inferences
- Most promising: **offshore wind and cable UXO / boulder / debris screening**. Buyers are survey contractors (Fugro, Gardline, Ocean Infinity, Bibby HydroMap, etc.) and developers; review is a repeat task per campaign, and the "coverage map plus calibrated confidence" output maps to the risk-based "ALARP" style evidence regulators and insurers want. But the GEOMAR paper shows sonar-only AI is not enough for UXO (magnetometers matter), and incumbents (Seequent, ALM) already sell target-list tools, so competition is real.
- Plausible second: **search-and-recovery / archaeology** where "how well was each area searched" is the core question (MH370 shows coverage claims matter), but budgets are sporadic. Useful as a demo or credibility segment, not the revenue base.
- Least promising: **defence MCM** (ATR well covered by Thales, SeeByte, Raytheon; classified data; procurement cycles) and **oil and gas** (not researched here; cannot rank it from evidence).
- Port/channel dredging, ghost gear, insurance/salvage: **not enough evidence to rank**.

### Gaps
- No direct evidence of willingness-to-pay for an AI review layer in any segment. The next step is interviews with 5-10 survey processors and party chiefs to get hours per line-km and per-project review budgets.
- Competitor landscape for sonar AI (e.g. SeeByte, Thales, Seequent, Kraken, others) was not mapped here beyond the names above.
- Oil and gas, dredging, archaeology, environmental/ghost-gear and insurance/salvage segments were not searched.
