## 1. Verification of the Stolte et al. 2022 figures

Source: Stolte A., Dederer G., et al. (2022), "The quest for ghost gear in the German Baltic Sea: A team effort between WWF, divers, fisherfolk, and public authorities", Front. Mar. Sci. 9:981840, published 07 Oct 2022 (Brief Research Report). Full text read from the Frontiers PDF [READ]: https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/marine-science/articles/10.3389/fmars.2022.981840/pdf (HTML: https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/marine-science/articles/10.3389/fmars.2022.981840/full)

### Takeaway
Both previously unverified figures ARE in the paper. "EUR 140 per hectare" is the paper's stated lower bound for a professional scooter-diver search; "5 diving days for 49 positions" is a compressed reading of "22 of 49 sonar positions verified during 5 diving days were confirmed as ALDFG" (and Table 1 lists 49 diver-verified positions for the Bay of Lübeck). The earlier "not verifiable" result was a retrieval failure (the figures sit in the Methods/Results text, not in the abstract). Both need careful wording because 140 EUR/ha is an authors' back-of-envelope estimate, not a measured cost.

### Cited Findings (exact quotes) [READ]
- Cost, diver search (the 140 figure): "This cost has to be compared to a full scientific diving team with a maximum area coverage of 14.4 ha/day with scooters with at least 3 divers and a skipper, where professional costs depend on country and region and in Germany, are typically between 2000 and 4000 Euros/day or at least 140 Euros per hectar."
- Cost, sonar: "Assuming a smaller vessel cost of at most 1000 Euros/130 ha results in a cost efficiency of 8 Euros per hectar."
- Comparison: "The sonar search turns out to be 12 times more efficient in time and 17 times more efficient in cost than the diver search, in addition to the larger area covered by sonar charting."
- Time efficiency: "The sonar is operated for 4-5 hours during a typical sonar cruise, implying an efficiency of 4.5h/130 ha or 2 minutes per hectar compared to 25 min/ha for 6 diving hours with scooters."
- Dive coverage (non-scooter): "In a circular area around a single point, a one-hour scientific dive covers a radius of approximately 30m and a search area of 2827m2. Rounding this to 3000m2, a day search with a rotating scientific diving team of four divers and six dives yields an area coverage of 18.000m2, less than 2 hectares or just 1.5% of the average daily sonar area."
- Scooter assumption: "Assuming a strip length of 1km and visibility of 3m as an upper limit in the Baltic summer months implying a strip width of 6m, a single dive might cover 4 strips or an area of 24.000m2. If six dives can be achieved, a total area of 14.4 ha can be covered in a single day during scooter searches. or just 11% of the average search area of 130 ha/day with the sonar".
- "5 diving days / 49 positions": "In a testbed area (Figure 4) near Neustadt, SH, after 3 days of sonar charting, 22 of 49 sonar positions verified during 5 diving days were confirmed as ALDFG, a success rate of 45%. All 22 nets could be retrieved within 9 recovery days with scientific divers." Figure 4 caption: "Sonar charting of 3 days led to 49 suspect positions in this test area, of which 22 were confirmed as lost gear or ropes on 5 scientific diving days. All 22 ALDFG could be retrieved on 9 days of recovery operations." Table 1, Bay of Lübeck row: 504 ha, 6 days at sea, 63 sonar suspect positions, 49 diver-verified.
- Totals confirmed: "The total charted area was 5820 ha on 45 days at sea" (Fig. 3 caption); Table 1 total row: 5820 ha, 45 days, 549 suspect positions, 176 diver-verified; "94 ALDFG" retrieved (Table 1 shows "94 (86)"); "the total success rate is 52%"; average "130 ha / day". Schleswig-Holstein: "326 suspect positions ... of which 93 were verified until February 2022, and while 40 were ALDFG (success rate 43%), 53 were other objects or active nets (false suspect rate 57%, Table 1)."
- Why suspects were false (the ONLY explanation given): "The interpretation of sonar data is limited especially in areas with soft seabed habitats, where the sound penetration into the sediments delivers a similar reflectivity signal as a larger lost trawl net. Structured seafloors with rich underwater flora, but also natural structures such as edges and reefs can render data interpretation complex."
- Operator skill: "teams with less experience in the interpretation of sonar scan data cannot expect to obtain similarly high recovery rates." Comparison to Puget Sound: "With more than 25 years of experience, their detection rate for lost traps, pots and gillnets is very high."
- Limits of method: North Sea pilot: "No lost trawl netting could be identified in 8 days at sea during this pilot sonar search"; "good knowledge of lost gear hot spots from regional fisheries is still a prerequisite for a successful and efficient search and retrieval campaign."
- Sonar spec: "Towing a Marine Sonics ArcExplorer sonar fish with a transponder frequency of 600kHz as low as 5m above the seafloor at a speed of 3-4 knots, the obtained sonar spatial resolution of a few centimetres is sufficient to detect gillnet lines as thin as 1cm and other lines from trawl netting, as well as fish traps (Figure 2)." "The swath width of 100m".
- Figure 2 caption: "(B-D) examples of sonar images of two gillnets and one trawl bundle showing its height above the seafloor by its extended sonar shadows".
- Verification: "Positions are visually identified during post-processing data analysis and need to be verified by divers."
- Trawl netting "is found in the Baltic Sea in large bundles weighing 1-4 tonnes each." Retrieved gear from Rügen included "trawl netting, mixed with other forms of fisheries and marine litter, including metals, anchors, cables, tires, and gillnets".

### Inferences
- **[INFERENCE]** The 140 EUR/ha is consistent with 2,000 EUR/day divided by the 14.4 ha/day scooter scenario (about 139 EUR/ha), and 17x = 140/8. It is a lower bound for the best-case scooter scenario. With the non-scooter 1.8 ha/day figure the same day-rate would be roughly 1,100+ EUR/ha. A NetSight citation must say "at least ~EUR 140/ha (authors' estimate, scooter-diver scenario, Germany)" and not "diver search costs 140/ha".
- The paper gives NO per-suspect breakdown of false-suspect causes (no counts of rocks vs. wrecks vs. active nets); Fig. 4 is annotated with "type of objects found" but those labels are in the image, not the text. The explanation of false suspects is generic (soft-seabed reflectivity, structured seabed). So any claim like "X% of false positives were boulders" cannot be attributed to Stolte.
- Possible internal inconsistency to avoid repeating: the Lübeck row/ caption say 22 ALDFG and "All 22 retrieved", but the extracted Table 1 cell reads "22 (20)" (retrieved count in brackets). Combined total is 94 found, 86 retrieved. Use 94 found / 86 retrieved. The text says "94 ALDFG to be retrieved" loosely.
- The 57% false-suspect rate applies to Schleswig-Holstein verified positions only; combined verified success is 52% (so combined false-suspect rate among verified is 48%, [INFERENCE] from 52%). 94/176 = 53.4%, and the paper says 52%; minor discrepancy, quote the paper's 52%.
- Verified positions are a biased sample (only suspects chosen for diving were verified: 176 of 549). So 52% is a precision-among-verified, not precision overall.

### Gaps
- Fig. 4 object-type labels (images only) not readable in text.
- No source for diver-day costs beyond the authors' range "2000 and 4000 Euros/day".

## 2. How ghost nets, trawl nets, gillnets, pots and lines look in side-scan sonar

### Takeaway
Published evidence supports: (a) high-frequency (600 kHz) towed sonar flown ~5 m above the seabed resolves 1 cm gillnet lines and trawl-net lines; (b) trawl-net bundles (1-4 t) are visible with height shown by shadows; (c) pots show bright returns with elongated shadows; (d) soft seabed, reefs, rock and flora produce confusing look-alikes and the false-suspect rate is high (57% in SH). I found NO published statement that "collapsed nets cast no shadow". The claim is physically plausible for flat nets but is unsupported by sources I could read, and Stolte's own Fig. 2 shows shadow-bearing net bundles. Treat "no shadow" as a hypothesis, and make shadow a soft feature, never a hard filter.

### Cited Findings
- GhostNetZero (Microsoft AI for Good Lab, WWF Germany, Fenn Enterprises, Accenture) [READ]: https://ecoevorxiv.org/repository/object/10294/download/19034 . Baltic data "Marine Sonic Mark II system operating at a 600 kHz frequency", 2019-2023, "depths of 5 m to 18 m", "239 representative images"; Puget Sound "Sea Scan PC side-scan sonar system (Marine Sonic) operating at a frequency of 600 kHz", 2014, to "approximately 36 m (120 ft)", "173 discrete targets". Model: DeepLabV3 + ResNet50 binary segmentation of "ghost net regions". Splits: Baltic 205 train / 34 validation; Puget 145 / 28; "No separate test set was allocated." Results: "BS+PS ... centroid detection rates of 0.891-0.929 for the 20px buffer across both regions"; at 3 px buffer BS 0.761, PS 0.821; mean IoU 0.739 (BS) and 0.685 (PS); BS-only model on Puget: 0.607 at all buffers (poor transfer). Abstract: "approximately 90% ghost net detection rate". The preprint reports no precision/false-positive rate and no per-class results. Future work: "expand the annotation categories to include these additional lost gears, such as crab pots." The preprint says images were converted to grayscale, split left/right, patches 2000 x 500 px. I did not see a posting date in the extracted text.
- Stolte 2022 [READ] (see section 1): lines as thin as 1 cm detected at 600 kHz, 5 m altitude, 100 m swath; net bundle height shown by "extended sonar shadows"; false-suspect causes in soft seabed and structured seafloor.
- NOAA/Northwest Straits/Snohomish crab-pot surveys [SEARCH-ONLY]: pots are "readily identifiable" in sidescan; sport pots (square) vs commercial pots with attached line (round) differ in shape; survey 50 m each side with a 10 m nadir blind spot at ~2.5 knots; in Port Susan 2006 "69 targets were investigated but nothing was found" (buried/rusted); 2021 "nine (9) crab pot targets were investigated but nothing was found"; about 41 pots/km2 estimated in 2003 Snohomish. https://www.snohomishcountywa.gov/Archive.asp?ADID=2091 ; https://www.stillaguamish.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/06/Port-Susan-2021-Derelict-Fishing-Gear-Project-Final-Report.pdf . From the Clallam County 2003 NRC final report [READ, text extracted from PDF]: survey with "A Marine Sonic Sidescan Sonar System operating at 600 kHz"; "No gillnets were observed during the derelict fishing gear survey either by the sidescan sonar survey or by the divers." https://nwstraits.org/sites/default/files/2025-10/cla-2003-0009-derelict-gear-removal-nrc-final-report.pdf . The report contains no description of shadow/brightness for nets.
- Massachusetts DMF lobster-pot sonar study [SEARCH-ONLY summary of the report]: "pots and rocks can be hard to differentiate in the sonar imagery and acoustic shadows from rocks can hide pots"; better on featureless bottom; Klein System 3000 dual-frequency. https://www.mass.gov/doc/development-of-side-scan-sonar-methodology-to-survey-derelict-lobster-pots-2017/download (HTTP 403 to my fetch; NOAA MDP summary page read: https://marinedebris.noaa.gov/prevention/estimating-abundance-derelict-lobster-pots-new-england-using-side-scan-sonar , which gives no detection rates).
- Washington DNR deepwater nets survey [SEARCH-ONLY; PDF too large to fetch]: sidescan can identify derelict net targets in rockfish habitat "with some limitations"; vertical hard substrate can mask net features and cracks in rock walls can look like nets. https://Fisheries.noaa.gov/s3/dam-migration/deepwater_nets_surveys_dnr.pdf
- GhostVision, J. Mar. Sci. Eng. 14(10):951 (2026) [SEARCH-ONLY, publisher returned 403]: search snippet says crab pots "bright returns and elongated acoustic shadows"; appearance "vary substantially with platform, frequency, gain settings, and environmental conditions"; Chesapeake study "94% (16 of 17) of a subset of side-scan targets ... correctly identified as derelict blue crab traps"; another survey "only one item was confirmed retrieved from 161 identified targets". These come from an AI-generated search summary, so verify before citing. https://doi.org/10.3390/jmse14100951
- Operator experience (WWF Germany) [SEARCH-ONLY via search summary]: the operator "must be trained and have enough experience to recognize a potential ghost net in the side-scan sonar images at various angles", sometimes several passes.
- Sonar resolution for NOAA survey use [READ]: HSSD 2022 §6.1.2.1: "capable of detecting an object on the sea floor that measures 1 m x 1 m x 1 m from shadow length measurements"; §6.1.2.3 towfish height 8-20% of range scale; HSSD 2026.0.02: maximum allowable range scale 100 m for NOAA ISD surveys. These are for navigation-hazard objects, not nets.

### Inferences
- **[INFERENCE] on the "collapsed net casts no shadow" claim:** Shadow length scales with object height and range (geometry; NOAA HSSD itself computes target height "based on side scan sonar shadow lengths"). A net lying flat will have a short or absent shadow, especially at far range and with a high towfish. So "little or no shadow, range- and altitude-dependent" is defensible; "no shadow, therefore shadow-required filters delete it" is a valid reasoning about HSSD-style filters, but "collapsed nets cast no shadow" as a universal statement is not supported by any published study found, and a bundled/snagged trawl net does produce shadow (Stolte Fig. 2). Recommend wording: "a collapsed or flattened net may produce little or no acoustic shadow; detection then relies on backscatter texture and shape".
- HSSD 2022 §6.1.3.3 explicitly allows picking low-height contacts: "Other contacts may be picked if the sonargram signature (e.g., size, shape, or pattern qualities) is notable." This is useful support for a no-shadow-required contact picker.
- 600 kHz at ~5 m altitude is the evidence base for 1 cm lines. Common 100/400-500 kHz survey sonar at 10-20 m altitude should not be assumed to resolve mesh or thin ropes; no source I read gives a mesh-resolution threshold (see Gaps). Net mesh itself was never claimed visible in any source read; what is visible is the net as a textured/linear/ bundled patch and its lines.
- Both published AI efforts are tiny (239 + 173 training/validation images; no test set), so NetSight's "calibrated probability" claim is novel relative to GhostNetZero, which reports only validation centroid detection rate with no precision.

### Gaps
- No published quantitative sonar signature (dB, texture statistics, shadow length) for collapsed vs standing vs snagged gillnets. No study read that defines "standing" vs "collapsed" net states in sonar. The three net states are NetSight's own construct; no source defines them.
- No source with frequency vs mesh-visibility threshold. Ropes/float-sink lines: only Stolte's 1 cm at 600 kHz, 5 m altitude.
- NOAA derelict-trap sonar reports with detection probabilities: the Mass. DMF report returned HTTP 403; the DNR PDF exceeded the fetch size limit; the ResearchGate paper "Evaluating the use of side scan sonar for improved detection and targeted retrieval of ALDFG" (https://www.researchgate.net/publication/372158149) and GGGI methods were not read.
- Ghost Fishing (NGO), WWF reports other than Stolte: not read.

## 3. Post-disaster debris in sonar and a realistic disaster taxonomy

### Takeaway
Public sources are thin on how each debris type looks. The best documented facts: Gulf (Katrina/Rita) debris targets were interpreted from side-scan imagery by NOAA Office of Coast Survey contractors, recorded with height/length/width, least depths and a danger-to-navigation flag, with no formal classification scheme in the public metadata; Tohoku surveys used side-scan to map position, number and shape of debris; the Japan Coast Guard found vehicles and huge caissons. I found no source for Hurricane Harvey/Ian or Sandy classification schemes. A disaster taxonomy therefore has to be NetSight's own, built on object-physics classes and flagged as such.

### Cited Findings
- Gulf of Mexico Marine Debris Project (Katrina/Rita) metadata [READ via fetch summary]: data "derived from interpretation of sidescan sonar imagery by contractors to NOAA's Office of Coast Survey"; fields: height, length, width (m and ft), charted/echosounder/estimated least depth, estimated clearance to surface, volume, "DTON_Y_N" ("Danger to navigation"), IMAGE (sidescan thumbnail name), REMARKS; no category domain; "not for navigation"; "interpretation ... of unknown provenance"; "No formal spatial accuracy assessment has been conducted". https://www.gsa.state.al.us/apps/CAGDV/Metadata/Infrastructure/gommdp_debris_locations.html
- NOAA ORR: Phase I "areas off the coast of Alabama, Mississippi, and eastern Louisiana were surveyed with side scan sonar ... survey teams used multi-beam survey technology for major targets" [SEARCH-ONLY]; >1,500 square nautical miles surveyed, data record "item location, dimensions, depth, and type as identified by sidescan sonar" (NCEI accession 0176605) [SEARCH-ONLY]. https://response.restoration.noaa.gov/node/1594 ; https://ncei.noaa.gov/archive/accession/0176605 . The "type" labels were not retrievable.
- Sandy: NOAA survey ships sent to New York Harbor to find "sunken containers and debris" with side scan and multibeam [SEARCH-ONLY]: https://maritime-executive.com/article/noaa-surveys-ports-and-channels-for-sandy-s-dangers
- NOAA 2018 survey off North Carolina: side scan flagged 61 contacts associated with lost containers, multibeam used for depths [SEARCH-ONLY]: https://www.marinelink.com/news/responders-containers434953?cid=55
- Tohoku 2011: Onagawa Bay side-scan project "A position, a number and a shape of the debris remained in Onagawa Bay is investigated using side scanning sonar" [SEARCH-ONLY]: https://www.jamstec.go.jp/i-teams/plan_result/e/0174_P01_en.html . JAMSTEC/Tokai Univ. debris mapping in Hirota, Ofunato, Otsuchi bays used side-scan, multibeam, sub-bottom, ROV [SEARCH-ONLY]: https://www.jamstec.go.jp/i-teams/plan_result/e/0132_R01_en.html . Japan Coast Guard: seabed image "believed to show a submerged vehicle"; caissons "boxes measuring 20 meters per side" [SEARCH-ONLY, news archive]: https://blog.geogarage.com/2014/11/nautical-charts-to-be-revised-to.html
- Not found: any Hurricane Harvey or Ian sonar classification; any tree/vegetation, building-rubble signature description.

### Recommended disaster-management taxonomy (proposal) [INFERENCE, informed by the above]
Group by what matters for response (navigation hazard, retrieval equipment, ferrous/UXO-like, environmental) rather than by claimed identity:
1. `vessel_wreck` (sunken boat/ship; hull-like, linear, high relief)
2. `container_box` (regular rectangular, ~6/12 m; relevant to Sandy, NC container loss)
3. `vehicle_like` (car/truck-sized compact object; Tohoku)
4. `large_structure_block` (caisson, concrete slab, pier section; Tohoku caissons)
5. `rubble_field` (cluster of many small hard targets; buildings/foundation debris) with an extent polygon instead of single point
6. `pipe_cable_linear` (linear, thin; includes hose/chain)
7. `tree_log_vegetation` (elongated, tapering, often with branches; unverified signature, mark low confidence)
8. `net_rope_gear` (ghost gear family, subclasses below)
9. `natural_rock_boulder`
10. `unknown_manmade_contact` and `unknown` (explicit abstention classes)
Rule: each class carries `confidence`, and ambiguous classes are merged to a parent (e.g. `vessel_wreck` vs `container_box` merges to `large_manmade_object`) when classifier probability for the split is below a stated threshold.

## 4. Recommended class taxonomy for NetSight (ghost gear + general) with net-state definitions

### Takeaway
The proposal's classes (shipwreck, pipe_cable, cylinder, compact_debris, net_standing, net_collapsed, rock) are a reasonable start but (1) lack a "net_snagged_or_bundle" state, whose evidence is the best (Stolte trawl bundles), (2) lack trap/pot, line/rope and abstention classes, and (3) have no S-57 mapping. Use a two-level taxonomy: a coarse "detection family" plus a fine label, and always allow `unknown`.

### Proposed taxonomy (labels are NetSight's design; evidence column cites what supports each)
| Label | Definition | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| `net_bundle_or_mound` | Trawl/gill net in a heap, tangled or snagged on a structure; stands proud of seabed | Stolte Fig. 2: "one trawl bundle showing its height above the seafloor by its extended sonar shadows"; bundles "weighing 1-4 tonnes" (READ) |
| `net_flat_or_collapsed` | Net lying slack/flattened on seabed; low relief; little or no shadow expected; identified by backscatter texture/ patch shape/ trailing lines | No direct source. Stolte: soft sediments give "similar reflectivity signal as a larger lost trawl net" (READ), which also shows the main confuser. [INFERENCE] for shadow behaviour |
| `net_standing_or_suspended` | Gillnet held up by floats/ leadline, vertical curtain with shadow | Only indirect: gillnet sink- and swimlines detected (Stolte). No sonar signature study found. Treat as hypothesis |
| `line_rope` | Linear thin feature, float/sink line, trawl line | Stolte: "gillnet lines as thin as 1cm and other lines from trawl netting" at 600 kHz |
| `trap_pot` | Crab/lobster/fish trap | Stolte (fish traps); Northwest Straits (pots identifiable) [SEARCH-ONLY]; Mass. DMF (pots vs rocks confusion) |
| `wreck_or_large_hull` | Vessel/aircraft/large structure | HSSD feature classes; S-57 WRECKS |
| `pipe_cable_linear` | Pipeline/cable/chain | S-57 OBSTRN / cable classes (not retrieved) |
| `compact_debris` | Small compact manmade (anchor, tire, drum) | Stolte list: "metals, anchors, cables, tires" |
| `rock_boulder` | Natural | confuser per Stolte, Mass DMF, WA DNR |
| `natural_seabed_texture` | Reef edge, flora, sediment change (hard negatives) | Stolte: "edges and reefs", "rich underwater flora" |
| `unknown` | Cannot classify | required for calibrated outputs |

Net-state definitions to put in the labelling guide (keep to observables, not intent):
- Standing: height from shadow >= threshold and linear/ curtain geometry; confirmed only by diver/ROV imagery.
- Collapsed/flat: no measurable height from shadow (below HSSD-type height thresholds) but textured bright/dark patch with net-like edges or trailing lines.
- Bundled/snagged: height measurable, irregular mass, often at a structure.
Drop `cylinder` as a ghost-gear class unless needed for UXO/pipe use; for UXO markets keep `cylinder_like` as a shape descriptor, not a class (see section 6).

### Inferences
- The "shadow-required filters delete it" argument is correct for NOAA HSSD navigation picking (contact heights >= 1 m from shadow length) and for any hazard filter built on it, but HSSD also lets analysts pick on "size, shape, or pattern" so a no-shadow net is not forbidden by the standard.
- Ground truth is the bottleneck: the only large verified ghost-gear set is 176 diver-verified positions (Stolte) that are not public ("the sonar data employed in this article are proprietary to WWF Germany").

### Gaps
- No evidence for `net_standing_or_suspended` sonar signature. Recommend NetSight label it provisional and not claim detection performance.

## 5. Standards for contact outputs

### 5a. IHO S-44 (feature detection, search, position uncertainty)
Version caveat: I read **S-44 Edition 6, March 2020 "Preview PR"** [READ]: https://metanorma.github.io/mn-samples-iho/documents/s44/document.pdf . Edition 6.1.0 (September 2022) and 6.2.0 (published Oct 2024; adopted April 2025) exist per search results [SEARCH-ONLY]: https://iho.int/uploads/user/pubs/standards/s-44/S-44_Edition_6.1.0_Chinese_2024.pdf , https://iho.int/uploads/user/circular_letters/eng_2025/CL21_2025_EN_v1.pdf . I could not confirm that the 6.1.0/6.2.0 numbers match the Preview. Verify against the current official PDF before citing numbers.

Table 7-1 (Preview, 95% confidence) [READ]:
| Order | Depth THU | Feature detection | Feature search | Bathymetric coverage |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Exclusive | 1 m | Cubic features > 0.5 m | 200% | 200% |
| Special | 2 m | Cubic features > 1 m | 100% | 100% |
| 1a | 5 m + 5% of depth | Cubic features > 2 m down to 40 m; 10% of depth beyond 40 m | 100% | 100% |
| 1b | 5 m + 5% of depth | Not specified | Recommended but not required | 5% |
| 2 | 20 m + 10% of depth | Not specified | Recommended but not required | 5% |
(Note: the table's "Depth THU" row is horizontal uncertainty of depth measurements. The search-result slideshow listing "2 m for Special Order, 5 m + 5% depth, 20 m + 10% depth" matches.) Draft HSSC letter says the Order 1a depth limit for 2 m cubes was raised to 50 m in draft 6.0.0 [SEARCH-ONLY]; the Preview text I read says 40 m, so there is a draft-vs-final discrepancy to check.
Key text [READ]: "A cubic feature is used as a basic shape reference for a system feature detection ability and implies a symmetrical 3-D shape of six equal square sides." "However, no single survey system can guarantee detection of all features." "Specified feature detection abilities are not implicit determinations of what constitutes a hazard to navigation." Metadata minimum includes "Feature detection ability: In metres", "Feature search: % of survey area searched", "Uncertainties achieved (at 95% Confidence Level) ... THU and TVU", "Technique of vertical / depth measurement (e.g. ... side scan sonar ...)".
**Implication:** S-44 detection is defined for cubes; nets and ropes are not cubes. NetSight must not claim S-44 feature-detection order compliance for nets; it can report "detection ability for X m cube" only from a calibrated Pd map for cubes, and report net Pd separately.

### 5b. NOAA HSSD

### 5d. How contacts are dispositioned (NOAA)
Contact picked -> correlated with other coverage/ multibeam -> developed with MBES (least depth) -> included in Final Feature File as `descrp = New/Update` if it meets feature criteria, or reported as DTON if a hazard; charted features not found are "disproved" only with 200% SSS or 100% point cloud over the search radius (`descrp = Delete`); others "Retain" or "Not Addressed" [READ: HSSD 2022 §6.1.3.3-6.1.3.6, §7.3, 7.3.4; HSSD 2026 §7.5, 17.1-17.2].

## 6. UXO / offshore wind contact conventions

### Takeaway
CIRIA C754 text itself was not accessible. The one concrete, public, UK offshore wind UXO survey specification I read gives a minimum contact list and thresholds that are directly adoptable. NetSight outputs should include these fields and use "potential UXO (pUXO)" language only for ranked candidates, never "UXO" as an identification.

### Cited Findings
- CIRIA C754, "Assessment and management of unexploded ordnance (UXO) risk in the marine environment" (2015, Cooper & Cooke, with Royal HaskoningDHV and 6 Alpha Associates) [SEARCH-ONLY]: https://cis.ihs.com//CIS/document/313715 ; https://www.offshorewind.biz/?p=136903
- Cenos Offshore Wind Farm UXO Survey Specification (6 Alpha Associates for Flotation Energy, in the EIA) [READ]: https://marine.gov.scot/sites/default/files/cenos_eia_vol.4_-_a4_-_uxo_survey_specifications_redacted.pdf
  - Purpose: data "which meets the requirements to reliably detect potential UXO (pUXO) and meet the requirements for As Low As Reasonably Practicable (ALARP) Safety Sign-off."
  - Threat sizes: "The minimum size threat item detectable using practicable methods, is an SC-50, (50 kg), German aerially delivered HE Bomb; equating to approximately 25kg detectable ferrous metal." Magnetometer designed for "a minimum threat item with a ferrous mass of 313kg" (Mark XVII Mine).
  - MBES/SSS role: "to detect low ferrous content UXO that may be located upon on the seabed, such as LMA/LMB mines".
  - Contact list minimum: "A contact list detailing a minimum of: Target ID, Easting (m), Northing (m), Dimensions (Length, Width, Height), Classification, Confidence", plus "Associated contact imagery from SSS waterfall view"; SSS mosaics "exported at 0.1m resolution" (up to 1 m allowed with notice); footnotes: "Length of contacts shall be the longest dimension, and width measured perpendicular to length. Height is to be derived from the slant angle and range"; "Confidence value and criteria to be defined by the survey contractor, at low/medium/high levels, or similar"; positions "adjusted to MBES imagery, where visible in both datasets."
  - Pick threshold: "Contacts shall be picked that are larger than 1.0m in any dimension." and "The survey contractor will not substitute contact picks by classifying boulder fields in high density areas where UXO assessment is taking place. 6 Alpha will review these listings ... and may discount any targets which, based on professional experience, are unlikely to represent UXO threat items".
  - Pipeline: "A final pUXO Master Target List may be produced by 6 Alpha following further pUXO assessment" (the contractor delivers a target database of initial anomaly picks; the consultant produces the pUXO list).
  - Disclaimer text: "It does not constitute a warranty or guarantee, expressed or implied".
- Green Volt Appendix 5.4 uses a banded risk approach (pUXO avoidance or target investigation, cUXO removal); abbreviations not defined in the snippet [SEARCH-ONLY]: https://greenvoltoffshorewind.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/02/Appendix-5.4-Unexploded-Ordnance-Risk-Mitigation-Strategy.pdf

### Inferences
- NetSight should add to the schema: `pUXO_candidate` (bool), `ferrous_indication` (only if MAG co-registered; side-scan alone cannot determine ferrous content), `threat_size_class`, and a rule that the 1.0 m pick threshold and "no suppression of boulder fields" apply in UXO mode. In UXO mode the model must not auto-discard `rock_boulder` contacts; it can only rank.
- The Cenos "Classification" categories are not enumerated in the document; no standard UXO class list was found (Gap).
- Other terms commonly in this market (cUXO, "UXO-like", Survey Verification Test with surrogate targets): the Cenos document describes an SVT with surrogate items [READ, §7], but exact C754 category definitions are unverified.

### Gaps
- C754 text (target categories, "potential UXO" definition, target list template) not accessed (paywalled/ not retrievable).

## 7. Safe wording for coverage and clearance claims

### Takeaway
Industry and standards language avoids "clear"/"no objects" claims. Wording must tie any statement to a defined target size, sensor/coverage, and confidence, and say the output does not certify absence.

### Cited Findings (source language NetSight can echo)
- S-44: "no single survey system can guarantee detection of all features." and "Specified feature detection abilities are not implicit determinations of what constitutes a hazard to navigation." [READ] https://metanorma.github.io/mn-samples-iho/documents/s44/document.pdf
- NOAA HSSD: claims are tied to "the size of features claimed to be detected by the survey"; "Gaps in 200% side scan sonar coverage should be treated as gaps in coverage"; 100% SSS "insufficient to disprove a feature"; disproval requires "conclusive evidence of absence" under 200% SSS or 100% point cloud [READ: HSSD 2022 and 2026.0.02].
- UXO: ALARP framing: "reduce the UXO risk to ALARP"; "reliably detect potential UXO (pUXO)"; "does not constitute a warranty or guarantee" [READ, Cenos spec].
- GOMMDP: "not for navigation" and "No formal spatial accuracy assessment has been conducted" [READ via fetch summary].
- No source found containing the exact phrase "not a clearance certificate"; that phrase is my suggestion.

### Approved wording list [INFERENCE/DESIGN, each tied to a source above]
Use:
- "Probability of detection for a [size] cube/ net patch under the recorded survey conditions" (HSSD "claimed to be detected" logic)
- "Candidate contact", "potential ghost-gear contact", "potential UXO (pUXO) candidate, unverified" (Cenos pUXO)
- "Calibrated probability that this contact is [class], estimated on [dataset]; not a confirmed identification"
- "Contact positions are estimates with horizontal uncertainty of X m (95%)" (S-44)
- "Coverage gaps (holidays) are shown; areas with low Pd are not assessed as free of objects"
- "Supports prioritisation of diver/ROV verification and decreases residual risk; it does not eliminate it (ALARP language)"
- "Not for navigation. Not a clearance certificate. No survey system can guarantee detection of all features." (S-44 quote)
- "Absence of a contact at Pd = X for size Y is not proof of absence"
Avoid:
- "cleared", "clear of nets/ UXO", "safe to dredge/ anchor/ dive", "no objects present", "100% detection", "AI-verified", "confirmed ghost net" (before diver/ROV), "guaranteed".
- Do not use "conclusive evidence of absence" except when quoting the NOAA disproval standard; NetSight at 100% single-pass cannot meet it.
- Do not cite "~90% detection" for NetSight; GhostNetZero's 0.89-0.93 is a validation-set centroid rate with 20 px tolerance, no test set, no precision.
