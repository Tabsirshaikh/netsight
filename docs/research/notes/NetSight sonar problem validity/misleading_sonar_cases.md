# Misleading, false, and misinterpreted sonar detections: documented cases and false-alarm statistics

Research date: 2026-11-01. Scope note: about 29 search/fetch calls. Several fetches failed (ATSB pages timed out; one Kerala Kaumudi page returned HTTP 410). Items drawn only from search-result summaries rather than a fetched page are labelled "(search summary only)". Technical-cause claims (shadow, nadir gap, multipath, layback and so on) could not be sourced and are listed under Gaps, not as findings.

---

## Q1. Famous false-contact cases worldwide: natural features taken for man-made objects, spurious contacts, and coverage gaps / false all-clear

### Takeaway
Well-documented cases show sonar-only identification is routinely wrong or inconclusive until a second sensor (AUV camera, ROV, divers, excavator) confirms. Two clear false-positive cases are Deep Sea Vision's "Earhart plane" (a natural rock formation) and the Baltic Sea Anomaly (most likely a glacial feature, from a single poor image). MH370 shows cluster contacts that turned out to be an unrelated shipwreck, plus coverage gaps. USS San Francisco (2005) and USS Connecticut (2021) show the "false all-clear" risk of unsurveyed or uncharted seabed. Loch Ness (Deepscan 1987) shows contacts that stay unexplained.

### Cited Findings

**A. Deep Sea Vision / Amelia Earhart "plane" (2024), false positive: natural rock**
- Date and place: Deep Sea Vision (DSV) released a sonar image in January 2024 showing a plane-like shape, about 100 miles from Howland Island in the Pacific. On 6 Nov 2024 it posted that follow-up imagery showed a "natural rock formation", not Earhart's Electra. — [NBC News](https://www.nbcnews.com/news/us-news/image-believed-show-amelia-earharts-plane-was-rock-formation-not-crash-rcna181578)
- Sensor: a HUGIN 6000 AUV scanned the seafloor in 1,600 m sections. The NBC article does not give the sonar specification. — [NBC News](https://www.nbcnews.com/news/us-news/image-believed-show-amelia-earharts-plane-was-rock-formation-not-crash-rcna181578)
- What the truth turned out to be: DSV wrote "After 11 months the waiting has finally ended and unfortunately our target was not Amelia's Electra 10E (just a natural rock formation)." DSV says it has cleared nearly 7,700 square miles. — [Flying Magazine](https://www.flyingmag.com/sonar-image-once-thought-proof-of-missing-earhart-plane-is-actually-a-rock-formation/) (quote carried via a search summary of this page)
- Lesson: the reversal came from the company's own follow-up drone imagery, about 10 months after the sonar image. NBC reports no independent expert review. The company had debated releasing the image without independent confirmation. — [NBC News](https://www.nbcnews.com/news/us-news/image-believed-show-amelia-earharts-plane-was-rock-formation-not-crash-rcna181578)

**B. Baltic Sea Anomaly / Ocean X (June 2011), likely natural feature; unresolved**
- A treasure-hunting team (Peter Lindberg, Dennis Åsberg, OceanX) took a sonar image in the Gulf of Bothnia in June 2011. OceanX described a roughly 60 m circular object with possibly man-made features. A 2012 Daily Mail story fuelled "sunken UFO" speculation. — [Wikipedia: Baltic Sea anomaly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baltic_Sea_anomaly) (secondary, but cites Snopes, Live Science, Popular Mechanics and NBC News)
- Hanumant Singh (Woods Hole) called the image "virtually useless for identifying an undersea formation" and attributed its distortions to a cheap, improperly wired and calibrated instrument. Charles Paull (MBARI) suggested a rock outcrop, trawler-dropped sediment, or a school of fish. Göran Ekberg (Stockholm Maritime Museum): "A natural, geological formation can't be ruled out." Jarmo Korteniemi considers it a glacial drumlin, one of a group of similar mounds on the Bothnian Sea floor. — [Wikipedia: Baltic Sea anomaly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baltic_Sea_anomaly)
- Samples were granite, gneiss, sandstone and a loose basalt piece. Geologists noted they came from the seafloor near the object, not from the object itself. — [Wikipedia: Baltic Sea anomaly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baltic_Sea_anomaly)
- Search results returned no peer-reviewed geological study of the site. Treat "natural glacial feature" as the expert consensus view, not a verified identification. — [History UK](https://www.history.co.uk/articles/the-baltic-sea-anomaly-uso-or-natural-formation) (surfaced by search; page not fetched)

**C. MH370 search: a real-looking cluster that was an unrelated shipwreck**
- Date: reported 13 May 2015. The deep-tow system on MV Fugro Equator found a cluster of small sonar contacts in the southern search area, about 12 nm east of the 7th arc. The ATSB judged it "of potential interest but unlikely to be related to MH370." An AUV follow-up from Fugro Supporter showed an uncharted shipwreck at about 3,900 m. — [gCaptain](https://gcaptain.com/shipwreck-found-in-search-for-missing-mh370-aircraft)
- Quote from Peter Foley, ATSB Director of the Operational Search: "We were cautious about this", noting "multiple small bright reflections in a relatively small area of otherwise featureless seabed." — [gCaptain](https://gcaptain.com/shipwreck-found-in-search-for-missing-mh370-aircraft)
- The ATSB triages contacts into tiers. Class 1 contacts are "high interest" and warrant immediate investigation. More than 400 seabed features were reported in the lowest category. — [Airline Ratings / ATSB summaries](https://www.airlineratings.com/articles/mh370-no-significant-contacts-identified-in-area-containing-csiro-hotspot) (search summary only; ATSB fact sheet fetch timed out. ATSB page: https://www.atsb.gov.au/node/2275)
- SBS reported that independent analyst Williamson identified two sonar contacts as "bear[ing] all the hallmarks of a debris field". The ATSB said they were a very small sample of extensive data and that Williamson had not brought the analysis to the ATSB. This is a disputed external interpretation, not a confirmed contact. — [SBS](https://www.sbs.com.au/news/article/sonar-images-in-mh370-search-bear-all-the-hallmarks-of-a-debris-field/usuthsgtd) (title and search summary only; not fetched)

**D. MH370: acoustic pinger detections (April 2014), later discounted**
- Initial reading: the coordinating agency first said the signals were consistent with flight recorders. A US Navy official later said there was broad agreement that they came from some other man-made source unrelated to the jet, and suggested the ship or the towed pinger locator's own electronics. The Navy spokesman called the CNN comments "speculative and premature." The Australian agency said that, in its professional judgment, the area "can now be discounted as the final resting place of MH370." — [NPR, 29 May 2014](https://www.npr.org/sections/thetwo-way/2014/05/29/316988422/search-for-flight-mh370-black-boxes-ends-pings-discounted)
- Caveat: no formal ATSB finding on what produced the pings was located. The attribution to equipment noise is a Navy official's view. A claim that the pings were at the wrong frequency came from unnamed scientists and is unverified.

**E. MH370: coverage gaps (false all-clear risk)**
- The ATSB-commissioned 2022 Geoscience Australia data review covered about 4,900 km², 29% of the 17,000 km² area requested. Ocean Infinity's January 2018 data (Malaysian Government-contracted survey) were not reviewed because Geoscience Australia did not have access. — [ATSB, MH370 data review 2022 final report](https://atsb.gov.au/sites/default/files/2024-02/mh370-data-review-2022-final-report-v2.pdf) (via search summary; PDF not fetched)
- Independent analyst Victor Iannello's group flagged about 30.5 km² around S34.53° E93.84° as never scanned. It also said a high-priority area south of the LEP was missed by earlier ATSB and Ocean Infinity searches because of steeply sloping terrain. — [Airline Ratings](https://www.airlineratings.com/articles/incomplete-atsb-mh370-review-creates-disbelief) (secondary, advocacy-leaning; unverified by ATSB)

**F. USS San Francisco (8 Jan 2005), uncharted seamount; chart gap**
- The submarine struck a seamount at about 525 ft depth, roughly 360 miles southeast of Guam. The navigation chart in use lacked the seamount, but "Other charts in San Francisco's possession did, however, clearly display a navigation hazard in the vicinity of the grounding." The Navy report called the cause an "accumulation of errors" and failure to follow procedures. One sailor died and many were injured (sources differ on injury counts). — [Wikipedia: USS San Francisco (SSN-711)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USS_San_Francisco_(SSN-711)); other accounts via [Naval Institute (Australia)](https://navalinstitute.com.au/history-n-subs-escape-after-sea-mount-crash/)
- Lesson: this was a charting and navigation-procedure failure, not a sonar misinterpretation. It shows that a "clear on the chart" water column can hide survey-coverage gaps. I found no source describing a post-incident sonar survey.

**G. USS Connecticut (2 Oct 2021), uncharted seamount in poorly surveyed waters**
- The submarine struck an uncharted seamount in the Indo-Pacific. The Navy found the mishap preventable, with errors in navigation planning, watch-team execution and risk management. Its optimal route included both surveyed and unsurveyed areas. The bridge team noted discrepancies between instrument depth data and the chart, and the commanding officer did not conduct a detailed route review. Location is redacted; reporting places it in the South China Sea. — [Pacific Fleet / Navy release](https://www.cpf.navy.mil/newsroom/news/article/3040738/us-navy-releases-command-investigation-into-the-uss-connecticut-grounding/); summary via [Sea Power](https://seapowermagazine.org/u-s-navy-releases-command-investigation-into-uss-connecticut-grounding/) (search summaries; pages not fetched). One outlet gives the date as 2 September, which conflicts with 2 October.

**H. Loch Ness / Operation Deepscan (September 1987), unexplained sonar contacts**
- About 20 boats with echo sounders formed a "sonar curtain". Contacts stronger than fish echoes lay at depth. On revisits most were in the same place but three had disappeared. The Loch Ness Project concludes "The contacts remain unexplained." — [Loch Ness Project](https://lochnessproject.org/FIELDWORKGROUNDTRUTH/DEEPSCAN%20OP/DEEPSCAN%20OPERATION%20LOCH%20NESS.html)
- Press accounts list three contacts, with some presumed anomalies later attributed to tree stumps. That source is weaker. — [Science Focus](https://www.sciencefocus.com/nature/operation-deepscan-the-hunt-for-the-loch-ness-monster/) (search summary only)
- I found no reliable source attributing the Deepscan contacts to a thermocline or fish schools. Do not state that as fact.

**I. Titan submersible search (June 2023), ambiguous acoustic "banging"**
- A Canadian P-3 aircraft with sonar buoys recorded rhythmic banging about every 30 minutes. The US Coast Guard said it did not know the source and passed the data to Navy experts. Woods Hole's Carl Hartsfield noted that telling human-made sounds from natural ones is difficult. A defense official later suggested natural, biological or surface-vessel noise. This is passive acoustics, not imaging sonar. — [The National](https://www.thenationalnews.com/world/us-news/2023/06/21/banging-noises-inconclusive-as-search-for-titanic-submersible-continues/); [Yahoo/AP](https://ca.news.yahoo.com/search-underway-titanic-tourist-submersible-154316596.html)
- The debris was found by an ROV (Pelagic's Odysseus 6K from Horizon Arctic), about 1,600 ft off the Titanic's bow. I found no source describing side-scan sonar contributing to that find. — [Maritime Executive](https://www.maritime-executive.com/article/debris-confirms-catastrophic-failure-of-titan-submersible-near-titanic)

### Inferences
- A common pattern: a sonar-only "object" is announced, then a second sensor (AUV camera, ROV, drone) reclassifies it (Earhart, MH370 wreck). In the Earhart case the reclassification took about 10 months.
- Contacts with few features in featureless seabed, a single image, or poorly calibrated hardware (Baltic Anomaly) are especially prone to over-interpretation.
- A "false all-clear" often comes from charts and survey coverage, not from a sonar misreading (San Francisco, Connecticut). Any claim that a route is "surveyed and clear" needs checking for line spacing and coverage.
- Passive acoustic cues (MH370 pings, Titan banging) raised hope that later evidence did not support.

### Gaps
- No case found of a channel or wreck-site declared clear by side-scan and then hit by a vessel, apart from submarine chart gaps. Searches on UK MAIB or hydrographic-office uncharted-wreck groundings were not run. Gap.
- Fish schools, thermoclines and ripples mistaken for objects: no specific documented incident located. Only Paull's offhand "school of fish" suggestion for the Baltic image (see B).
- Shipwreck "discoveries" later shown to be rocks: no verified case beyond Earhart and the Baltic Anomaly.
- The ATSB MH370 sonar-contact pages (atsb.gov.au/node/2275 and the July fact sheet) timed out, so contact counts and tier definitions rest on secondary summaries.
- No verified 2025-2026 incidents were found in this pass.

## Q2. Indian cases and how reliable sonar was

### Takeaway
The best-documented Indian case is the Shirur / Gangavali (Karnataka) search for Arjun's lorry after the 16 July 2024 landslide. Early radar, sonar, IBODS and Ferrex-locator signals led to a ministerial announcement on 24 July that the truck was found. The truck was in fact recovered only on 25 September 2024, 72 days after the landslide, after several leads proved wrong. A positive Indian counterexample is the IAF An-32, found by NIOT multibeam/SAS in 2024. Other Indian cases on the brief (Dwarka, Hooghly, Krishna/Godavari bridges, Bombay High, Kerala/TN wrecks) were not researched.

### Cited Findings

**Shirur / Gangavali, Karnataka (16 July to 25 Sept 2024)**
- 16 July 2024: a landslide on NH 66 swept away Arjun and his lorry. Radar picked up three signals at the site on 20 July. On 23 July sonar picked up a signal in the river at the same spot where radar had shown one. — [Onmanorama 72-day timeline](https://www.onmanorama.com/news/kerala/2024/09/25/72-days-search-arjun-timeline-events-kozhikode-truck-body-gangavali.html)
- 23 July: the Navy's sonar "receives strong signal", with the Army saying the large object could be either Arjun's lorry or a big mobile tower. — [Kerala Kaumudi](https://keralakaumudi.com/en/news/india/general/presence-of-large-object-detected-navys-sonar-receives-strong-signal-from-river-1350281) (search summary only; the "mobile tower" alternative comes from an aggregated search summary)
- 24 July: outlets reported the truck found submerged in the Gangavali after radar and sonar signals. Karnataka Revenue Minister Krishna Byre Gowda confirmed it was detected. — [Onmanorama, 24 July](https://onmanorama.com/news/kerala/2024/07/24/ankola-shirur-landslide-truck-located-arjun-search-karnataka.html); [The News Minute](https://www.thenewsminute.com/kerala/karnataka-landslide-kerala-man-arjuns-lorry-found-in-gangavali-river)
- 25 July: Onmanorama reported the truck was traced using IBODS (a drone-based buried-object detector) and a Ferex locator, about 20 m from the bank and 5 m underwater. Heavy rain and strong currents halted operations. It stated that whether Arjun was inside the truck was still unknown. — [Onmanorama, 25 July](https://onmanorama.com/news/kerala/2024/07/25/shirur-landslide-search-continues-for-arjun-after-tracing-truck.html)
- The same day, the Army's ferromagnetic locator reportedly found three spots, and officials still had to work out which was the cabin. — [Onmanorama / Kerala Kaumudi search summary](https://www.onmanorama.com/news/kerala/2024/07/25/ankola-landslide-shirur-karnataka-drone-boom-excavator-equipments-used.html) (search summary only)
- 27 July: the Navy focused on four spots where the drone had captured metallic and radio-frequency signatures. 31 July: phase one suspended for rain and strong current. — [Onmanorama 72-day timeline](https://www.onmanorama.com/news/kerala/2024/09/25/72-days-search-arjun-timeline-events-kozhikode-truck-body-gangavali.html)
- 13-14 August: the lorry's hydraulic jack, a wooden door and a rope were recovered (the owner identified the rope). — [Onmanorama 72-day timeline](https://www.onmanorama.com/news/kerala/2024/09/25/72-days-search-arjun-timeline-events-kozhikode-truck-body-gangavali.html)
- August: a metal part recovered by diver Eshwar Malpe was reported as not belonging to Arjun's lorry, likely to another vehicle in the accident. — [Kerala Kaumudi](https://keralakaumudi.com/web-news/en/2024/08/NMAN0521379/1.html) (search summary only; the page returned HTTP 410 on fetch)
- 21 September: a steering wheel, clutch and tyre parts recovered with a dredger were first thought to be from Arjun's lorry. The owner and the local MLA said none of them came from it, and Onmanorama says this was later "confirmed otherwise". — [Onmanorama 72-day timeline](https://www.onmanorama.com/news/kerala/2024/09/25/72-days-search-arjun-timeline-events-kozhikode-truck-body-gangavali.html); [Onmanorama, 21 Sept](https://onmanorama.com/news/kerala/2024/09/21/search-for-arjun-resumes-no-remains-of-his-truck-found.html) (search summary)
- 25 September: the lorry was found in the Gangavali at one of the four points shortlisted, with body parts suspected to be Arjun's in the cabin (DNA pending at that time; the News Minute reported remains returned home 75 days after the landslide). — [Onmanorama 72-day timeline](https://www.onmanorama.com/news/kerala/2024/09/25/72-days-search-arjun-timeline-events-kozhikode-truck-body-gangavali.html); [The News Minute](https://www.thenewsminute.com/kerala/kerala-truck-driver-arjuns-mortal-remains-return-home-75-days-after-landslide)
- The Karnataka High Court sent notices over alleged search lapses (24 July). Title only; content not read. — [Onmanorama](https://onmanorama.com/news/kerala/2024/07/24/shirur-landslide-karnataka-high-court-arjun-search.html)

**IAF An-32, Bay of Bengal (lost 22 July 2016; found January 2024), sonar success**
- A 2016 Navy and Coast Guard search found no debris and was called off 15 September 2016. In January 2024 NIOT located debris about 310 km off Chennai at about 3,400 m, using an underwater vehicle with multibeam sonar, synthetic aperture sonar and high-resolution photography. The IAF confirmed the images conform to an An-32. — [ETV Bharat](https://www.etvbharat.com/english/national/bharat/niot-locates-iaf-an32-aircraft-debris-in-seabed-off-chennai-coast-seven-years-later/na20240112173650005005564); [The Week](https://www.theweek.in/news/india/2024/01/12/over-seven-years-later-wreckage-of-missing-iaf-an-32-traced-in-bay-of-bengal.amp.html); [Deccan Herald](https://www.deccanherald.com/amp/story/india%2Fwreckage-of-iafs-an-32-aircraft-traced-in-bay-of-bengal-more-than-7-years-after-it-went-missing-2846502)
- Some outlets say "possibly" the plane's wreckage; others report confirmation. — [The National](https://thenationalnews.com/world/asia/2024/01/12/india-missing-plane-mystery)

### Inferences
- Shirur: sonar, radar and metal-signature tools produced real hits but not a decisive identification. The key problem was that a "detected object" (lorry, tower, other debris) could not be told apart without physical recovery, in a fast-flowing, debris-laden river with strong currents. The 24-25 July announcements that the truck was "located" were not borne out until 25 September, and I found no source that says which July signal (if any) was the truck. I label the early announcement as premature, not as a verified sonar false positive.
- Shirur also shows that multi-sensor "confirmation" can be wrong when each sensor responds to metal in general (radar/locator/sonar), and that rope, door and jack fragments are the kind of ground-truth that settled things.
- The An-32 case shows that a modern multibeam/SAS survey can succeed where an earlier surface-led search did not. I cannot say from sources whether the 2016 search covered the 2024 location, so I cannot call it a missed detection.

### Gaps
- Not researched or no sources found: Dwarka submerged structures (NIOT/ASI), Kolkata Hooghly, Krishna/Godavari river bridge collapses, Mullaperiyar, Bombay High / ONGC wreck or body searches, Kerala and Tamil Nadu wreck searches. These remain open.
- Shirur: I could not find a report that explicitly says which sonar contact was the lorry and which were not. The "Navy found nothing" statements and the exact IBODS/Ferex outcome of 25 July are not documented in fetched sources. The IBODS system name appears only in the Onmanorama 25 July article.
- A reported November 2024 repeat sonar inspection at the marked location was seen only in a search summary and is not included as a finding.

## Q3. Quantitative false-alarm evidence

### Takeaway
The only hard numbers found are from the WWF Baltic ghost-gear study: 549 sonar suspect positions, 176 diver-verified, 94 confirmed as lost fishing gear (about 53%, reported as 52%), leaving 82 verified positions that were not ghost gear. Per-km² mine-hunting false-alarm rates were not found. Literature says only that ATR false alarms remain too high for modern mines and degrade in complex terrain. No sonar-specific expert-vs-expert disagreement study was found.

### Cited Findings
- Stolte et al. 2022 (Frontiers in Marine Science, "The quest for ghost gear in the German Baltic Sea"): 549 sonar suspect positions (326 in Schleswig-Holstein, 223 in Mecklenburg-Western Pomerania); 176 verified by divers (93 and 83); 94 confirmed ALDFG (40 and 54); the paper reports "the total success rate is 52%". — [Frontiers](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fmars.2022.981840)
- Survey effort: 5,820 ha surveyed (4,425 ha SH, 1,395 ha MV) over 45 days at sea with a 100 m sonar swath. — [Frontiers](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fmars.2022.981840)
- Schleswig-Holstein: 40 of 93 verified were ALDFG, a 43% success rate. The non-gear positions were described as "other objects or active nets." — [Frontiers](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fmars.2022.981840)
- Cause of confusion given by the authors: "natural structures such as edges and reefs can render data interpretation complex", and soft seabeds can give reflectivity similar to a lost trawl net. — [Frontiers](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fmars.2022.981840) (paraphrase from fetch summary; verify wording in paper)
- ATR literature (SeeByte, arXiv 2404.18663): ATR performance on side-scan imagery degrades rapidly in non-benign (complex) environments. The paper proposes inserting simulated mine-like contacts to measure terrain complexity and an operator-labelled seabed clustering method. Authors Guerneve, Loizou, Munafo, Mignotte. — [arXiv 2404.18663](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.18663v1) (abstract-level; the quantitative results were not read)
- A 2008 ATR review: ATR works well for large WWII-era mines but "false alarm rates, however, are still unacceptably high for modern mines." — [Advanced ATR techniques based on High-Resolution SAS Sensors (JASA record)](https://bvbvf1.bib-bvb.de/vufind/PrimoRecord/cdi_crossref_primary_10_1121_1_2936050) (search summary)
- Older Heriot-Watt model-based study: 0.275 false alarms per image across 200 data files. Per-image rates do not convert to per-km². — [Heriot-Watt Sonar ATR](https://home.eps.hw.ac.uk/~ceeyrp/WWW/Research/SonarATR/outline1.html) (search summary)
- Seabed mapping classification depends on the interpreter's experience and area knowledge (2019 Brazilian study); no quantitative agreement figures. — [Revista Brasileira de Geofísica](https://sbgf.org.br/revista/index.php/rbgf/article/download/1985/1167) (search summary)
- Vendor claim, not independent evidence: Thales says its AUV SAMDIS SAS achieved high detection and classification confidence with a "very low false alarm rate", with no numbers. — [EDR Magazine](https://www.edrmagazine.eu/thales-successfully-completes-demonstration-of-its-exhaustive-mine-hunting-unmanned-package-in-belgium)

### Inferences
- Computed from the Stolte figures (my arithmetic, not stated in the paper unless noted): 176/549 = about 32% of suspects were verified; 94/176 = 53.4% (paper says 52%); 82/176 = about 47% of verified suspects were not ghost gear. Regional split: SH 43% (40/93), MV 65% (54/83).
- The 53% figure is a precision among checked suspects only. The 373 unverified suspects were not resolved, and the paper says nothing here about ghost gear missed by the sonar (recall). The real false-alarm share could differ, and verification priority may have biased the sample.
- Stolte's survey density (about 549 suspects over 5,820 ha, roughly 0.09 per ha, or about 9.4 per km²) is my calculation from the stated area and counts and covers suspects, not confirmed false alarms. At 47% false-positive share among verified suspects, that implies roughly 4 non-gear suspects per km² if the verified share is representative, which is uncertain.

### Gaps
- No published per-km² MCM false-alarm rates were located (MANEX'14 comparison paper, CMRE or NATO reports not retrieved).
- No expert-vs-expert disagreement study specific to side-scan sonar was located. Related optical-image studies exist (Progress in Oceanography 2024, eleven annotators; Beijbom et al. 2015) but are not sonar.
- No ATR "operator trust" literature was found in this pass.
- Rock-versus-ripple confusion rates: not found.

## Q4. Technical causes of misleading or missed detections

### Takeaway
Only a few causes are directly supported by sources found: natural seabed structure and soft sediment mimicking targets (Stolte), poor or uncalibrated hardware and single-image evidence (Baltic Anomaly), featureless seabed with small bright returns (MH370), terrain complexity degrading ATR (SeeByte), and steep terrain causing coverage gaps (MH370). The classic mechanisms in the brief (acoustic-shadow ambiguity, nadir gap, range-dependent resolution, towfish instability, multipath, speckle, sound-speed error, layback error) were not sourced in this pass.

### Cited Findings
- Natural edges, reefs and soft seabed produce signatures similar to lost nets. — [Frontiers](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fmars.2022.981840)
- Instrument quality: Singh attributed the Baltic image's distortions to a cheap, improperly wired and calibrated instrument. — [Wikipedia: Baltic Sea anomaly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baltic_Sea_anomaly)
- Contact semantics: "multiple small bright reflections in a relatively small area of otherwise featureless seabed" were enough to trigger an AUV follow-up. — [gCaptain](https://gcaptain.com/shipwreck-found-in-search-for-missing-mh370-aircraft)
- Steeply sloping terrain left a high-priority MH370 area unscanned. — [Airline Ratings](https://www.airlineratings.com/articles/incomplete-atsb-mh370-review-creates-disbelief) (secondary; advocacy-leaning)
- ATR degrades in non-benign terrain. — [arXiv 2404.18663](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.18663v1)
- Strong river currents and rain repeatedly halted the Shirur search, limiting verification. — [Onmanorama 72-day timeline](https://www.onmanorama.com/news/kerala/2024/09/25/72-days-search-arjun-timeline-events-kozhikode-truck-body-gangavali.html)

### Inferences
- Verification (diver, ROV, recovery) is the dominant defence against false positives in every case above. Sonar produces candidates, not identifications.
- Operating conditions (current, weather, debris) can block verification, which turned a quick "located" call at Shirur into a 72-day search.

### Gaps
- Acoustic shadow ambiguity, nadir gap, range-dependent resolution, towfish instability, multipath, speckle, sound-speed and layback errors: no source retrieved in this pass. A report writer should cite hydrographic standards or textbooks (for example IHO S-44 or sonar texts) rather than relying on these notes.
