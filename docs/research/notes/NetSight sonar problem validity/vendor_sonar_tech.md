## 1. What exactly does the Deep Trekker page claim, and what does it NOT say?

### Takeaway
The page is an educational/marketing explainer on sonar types and operator technique. It claims sonar helps locate and identify objects, but it is entirely operator-driven: it never mentions AI, automatic target recognition, statistical detection confidence, or probability of detection, and "coverage" is discussed only as a geometric altitude rule and a live "which areas have been scanned" display.

### Cited Findings
- Title "Sonar Systems Explained: Imaging, Side Scan, 3D & USBL", author Chad Gillen, published/updated September 1, 2026 — [Deep Trekker](https://www.deeptrekker.com/news/sonar-systems)
- Sonar types covered: echosounders/altimeters, CHIRP, Doppler velocity logs, USBL, side-scan, 2D imaging (multibeam/scanning), 3D imaging; active vs passive — [Deep Trekker](https://www.deeptrekker.com/news/sonar-systems)
- Deep Trekker models listed (all Blueprint Subsea Oculus-family multibeams): M370S (up to 200 m, low-resolution, long range, open ocean/shipwreck), M750D (dual-frequency, described as a best-seller), M1200D (2.1 MHz peak, 2.5 mm range resolution, close-range search/EOD/MCM), M3000D (highest frequency, short range, tunnels/tanks/hulls), C550D (compact budget, 100 m max, lower resolution); 2D imaging described generally as hundreds of beams in a 120 deg x 20 deg band; side-scan options for REVOLUTION and PIVOT ROVs; 3D imaging sonar via the SPECTRA/BRIDGE ecosystem with SLAM — [Deep Trekker](https://www.deeptrekker.com/news/sonar-systems); corroborated that Deep Trekker supports Oculus C550D/M370S/M750D/M1200D/M3000D, with data exported as .OCULUS for Blueprint's Viewpoint software (v1.18.243+) — [Deep Trekker support](https://www.support.deeptrekker.com/accessories/sonars/supported-sonars)
- Search and recovery claims: side-scan "help[s] locate bodies and guide divers"; "ROVs with imaging sonars offer a safe alternative to verify and recover the victim instead of divers"; 3D sonar "Real-time 3D visualization allows faster identification of objects or evidence"; USBL helps navigate large search areas — [Deep Trekker](https://www.deeptrekker.com/news/sonar-systems)
- Detection/classification claims: "Advanced modern tools can also identify an object's size, shape, and orientation in detail"; sonar can help identify size and composition; but sonar "cannot distinguish between overlapping objects at the same slant range"; rock/metal return strong echoes while sand/mud/silt/plants absorb; multibeam more likely to pick up marine life, bubbles, particulates "requiring more filtering" — [Deep Trekker](https://www.deeptrekker.com/news/sonar-systems)
- The word "debris" does not appear; closest are "Foreign objects lying on the seafloor" and point clouds measuring "foreign objects" in pipelines — [Deep Trekker](https://www.deeptrekker.com/news/sonar-systems)
- Manual/operator-skill statements: sonar use can be "more of an art than science in many cases"; finding small objects "requires practice"; operators must understand relative bearings; pilots must move slowly/hold position to avoid smear; altitude and angle adjusted by the operator; multibeam data "requires advanced data processing and specialized software"; a four-step manual method for locating an object (stabilise ROV, 360 deg polar scan, align to zero relative bearing, switch to ~90 deg sector scan) — [Deep Trekker](https://www.deeptrekker.com/news/sonar-systems)
- Acoustic shadows: low altitude/shallow angle gives long shadows that aid detection; steep angle/high altitude gives short shadows that help separate objects in clutter; wide shadows can hide neighbouring objects — [Deep Trekker](https://www.deeptrekker.com/news/sonar-systems)
- Coverage: the "10% rule" (altitude = 10% of range) yields "around 70% seabed coverage" (example: 10 m range -> 1 m altitude), called "one of the most reliable" guidelines (an opinion, no measurement); for 3D sonar operators "can visualize exactly which areas have been scanned"; SPECTRA + SLAM helps "verify coverage as the mission progresses"; "operational confidence" refers to operators confirming path/coverage during the dive — [Deep Trekker](https://www.deeptrekker.com/news/sonar-systems)
- Software: SoundTiles "automates" acoustic mosaics (stitches sonar images into a geo-referenced orthomosaic, used for dams, tunnels, hulls; can combine with 3D SLAM for 3D reconstructions). This is automated mosaicking, not target detection — [Deep Trekker](https://www.deeptrekker.com/news/sonar-systems)
- EOD/MCM workflow: towed/boat side-scan finds anomalies; an ROV with imaging sonar then re-identifies and approaches the target to decide if it is ordnance — [Deep Trekker](https://www.deeptrekker.com/news/sonar-systems)
- USBL "20 cm accuracy" cited without test conditions — [Deep Trekker](https://www.deeptrekker.com/news/sonar-systems)
- Explicit terminology check performed on the page: AI = not mentioned; automatic target recognition = not mentioned; probability of detection = not mentioned; "confidence" only as "operational confidence" (non-statistical); "coverage" appears as above — [Deep Trekker](https://www.deeptrekker.com/news/sonar-systems)
- Page-level links include a search-and-recovery tactics resource, dead-reckoning-101, a photogrammetry article, and the /industries/search-and-recovery page; these linked pages were NOT individually fetched. The linked product pages were represented on the page in the model table above — [Deep Trekker](https://www.deeptrekker.com/news/sonar-systems)

### Inferences
- Deep Trekker's own positioning treats sonar interpretation as an expert human task ("art", "practice"). Its automation is limited to mosaicking (SoundTiles), SLAM positioning and coverage display. The page is consistent with the premise that no automated detection is offered by Deep Trekker.
- The "~70% coverage" figure is a rule-of-thumb for geometry, not a measured probability of detection, and says nothing about the probability of finding a given object within the covered area.
- Because Deep Trekker resells Blueprint Oculus sonars, any detection capability would have to come from Blueprint/third-party software; none is claimed.

## 2. How advanced is commercial sonar technology (SAS, multibeam imaging, AI)? Is it much more advanced than classic side-scan?

### Takeaway
Yes at the high end: synthetic aperture sonar (SAS) delivers centimetre-class, range-independent resolution, and several vendors ship or announce ML target detection. But the advanced hardware (HISAS, Solstice, DUAL-SAS-class) sits on AUVs/naval-grade platforms, while ROV-class vendors such as Deep Trekker/Blueprint sell conventional multibeam imaging and side-scan with human interpretation.

### Cited Findings
- Kongsberg HISAS2020 is a platform-agnostic SAS (listed as "HISAS2020 ACR 80"), previously only available within HUGIN AUV integrated payloads, now sellable standalone for AUVs, USVs, ROVs, towfish, submarines. First trials on HUGIN Edge gave approx. 13 mm x 13 mm resolution. Article says it helps operators "detect smaller objects" and "classify targets with greater confidence" but gives no range, coverage rate, or AI features. Dated 2026 by URL/copyright only — [Kongsberg](https://www.kongsberg.com/news/news-archive/2026/validated-at-sea-hisas2020-delivers-on-its-promise/); also [Kongsberg](https://www.kongsberg.com/news/news-archive/2026/proven-synthetic-aperture-sonar-now-available-beyond-integrated-systems/) (snippet only)
- Sonardyne Solstice is a Multi Aperture Sonar (MAS, back-projection beamforming, 200 m swath, 0.15 deg along-track) for Search-Classify-Map and hydrographic AUVs; fitted as standard to General Dynamics Bluefin-9/-12; integrated in MINTACS mine-warfare software for the Royal Australian Navy in Sept 2020 (older source) — [ADS Group](https://www.adsgroup.org.uk/knowledge/industry-member-news/sonardyne-s-solstice-mcm-sonar-now-mintacs-compatible-for-ran/), [Wavefront 8200 Solstice datasheet](https://www.unmannedsystemstechnology.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/06/Wavefront_8200_Solstice-Data-Shet-1-1.pdf)
- Blueprint Subsea Oculus M-series: compact 2D multibeam imaging sonars (<400 g in water), 750 kHz/1.2 MHz dual-frequency models, update rates up to ~40 Hz; sources disagree on some range/FOV specs (e.g. M1200d range 40 m vs. others) — [Offshore Technology](https://www.offshore-technology.com/products/oculus-m-series/), [Maverick Drone listing](https://www.maverickdrone.com/products/3d-multibeam-sonar). Search found no evidence of AI detection in Blueprint's Viewpoint software (absence in snippets only; not confirmed from Blueprint's site)
- Teledyne: BlueView M900 Mk2 multibeam imaging sonar is used in research USV work (Chalmers thesis); SeaBat F50 forward-looking sonar showcased with FLIR camera for multi-sensor detection (Feb 2026 report; no automatic classification described) — [Chalmers ODR](https://odr.chalmers.se/items/8a7cea57-8075-424f-9159-4174e9e3165d/full), [Pulse (SeaSEC report)](https://www.pulse.bot/defense/news/teledyne-marine-showcased-advanced-sonar-autonomous-tech-at-seasec-harbour-challenge-415e74a9-6d23-408e-a91d-cdede530420d)
- EdgeTech: side-scan (4125, 2205 with Full Spectrum CHIRP, Dynamic Aperture Sonar) and a 2025-26 OEM side-scan expansion for micro-UUVs/small ROVs; no AI product found; a 2026 job posting mentions detection algorithm work (not a product) — [Dredging Today](https://dredgingtoday.com/?p=21529), [Marine Technology News](https://www.marinetechnologynews.com/news/c/resolution-sonar-imaging-systems)
- Klein (Exail group) 5900 side-scan paired with Exail navigation (May 2025 note); no AI claims found — [Marine Technology News search result](https://www.marinetechnologynews.com/news/capability-teledyne-gavia-604887) (only snippet seen)
- Exail: bought simulated sonar data from Mission Systems (S4 simulator) to train AI ATR classifiers for MCM; integration into UMISOFT planned; Australian SEA 1905 context (2023 article) — [Naval News](https://www.navalnews.com/event-news/indo-pacific-2023/2023/10/exail-buys-simulated-sonar-data-for-automatic-target-recognition/). I did not find DUAL-SAS/Shark-Hunter datasheets
- Recreational: Humminbird MEGA Side Imaging, Garmin SideVü/ClearVü, Garmin LiveScope, Humminbird MEGA Live; search found no AI fish/object detection claims and buying guides are affiliate-driven — [Field & Stream](https://www.fieldandstream.com/gear/best-side-imaging-fish-finders/), [Outdoor Life](https://www.outdoorlife.com/gear/best-side-imaging-fish-finders/)
- Not researched to evidence level: Norbit (only linked via Sonobot 5 datasheet snippet), Ping DSP, MarineSonic, QPS Qimera, Teledyne Oculus-vs-BlueView detail beyond the above

### Inferences
- Relative to classic side-scan, SAS is a step change in resolution and range-independence; MAS (Solstice) and 2D/3D multibeam imaging are further steps for near-field identification. The step change is in sensor quality, though, and mostly on defence/survey-grade platforms.
- ROV-class sonar (Oculus, Deep Trekker) is still essentially a "live imaging" tool for a human, not an automated search sensor.

### Gaps
- No datasheet-level confirmation of Teledyne Marine, Exail DUAL-SAS or Norbit specs in this pass; no check of Kongsberg's "proven SAS" article text beyond snippet.

## 3. Do any commercial products already provide AI/ATR, calibrated confidence, Pd/coverage maps, or false-alarm control for civilian debris/object search?

### Takeaway
ATR/ML detection exists commercially (Kongsberg SAS Target Assistant, SonarWiz 8.1 ATR, Charles River AutoTRap on Gavia, EvoLogics Sonobot object recognition, MINTACS/Solstice ATR, AquaEye handheld). Public evidence of calibrated confidence, Pd maps, or quantified false-alarm control is thin to absent; where numbers exist they are single-scenario vendor claims or academic prototypes.

### Cited Findings
- Kongsberg SAS Target Assistant (press release 5 March 2024), built with Cathx Ocean's Clarity Sensor Intelligence: runs inside Reflection PMA on HISAS data from HUGIN AUVs; users predefine object size/shape; "automatically detects and measures objects of interest and creates a target list for review/confirmation, further analysis, and export"; claims "consistent and reliable detection"; no confidence scores, Pd, false-alarm rate or coverage figures stated — [Kongsberg](https://www.kongsberg.com/news/news-archive/2024/sas-target-assistant/)
- Chesapeake SonarWiz 8.1.0 (Sea Technology, 30 Oct 2024): ATR "powered by an AI convolutional neural network" that can "Locate, classify and measure potential targets"; no confidence or coverage detail — [Sea Technology](https://sea-technology.com/sonarwiz-8-1-0). A third-party profile calls ATR a prototype with no published accuracy metrics (lower-quality source; conflicts with the release claim) — [robotics.press](https://www.robotics.press/companies/chesapeake-technology-inc/)
- Chesapeake SonarWiz Fathom (Feb 2026 vendor article): ML models "being developed" to separate mine-like objects from geology/debris/clutter; aims to "cut false alarm review by an order of magnitude while maintaining detection performance"; says "Detections carry probability estimates, high-confidence targets rise to the top" (no calibration method given); no Pd/validation data; describes legacy workflows as processors "slogging through hundreds of contacts" — [Maritime Magazines / Marine Technology](https://www.maritimemagazines.com/marine-technology/202602/the-future-of-sonar-processing)
- Charles River Analytics AutoTRap Onboard on Teledyne Gavia: real-time contact reports from side-scan; vendor-reported 90% probability of detection for truncated conical objects on a rocky volcanic seabed (Iceland, 10-30 m); no false-alarm figure published; first qualified on a Gavia in 2018 (older) — [Ocean Science & Technology](https://www.oceansciencetechnology.com/feature/charles-river-analytics-maritime-autonomy-technologies/), [Maritime Magazines (Oct 2020)](https://www.maritimemagazines.com/marine-technology/202010/the-journey-to-helping-auvs-think)
- Teledyne Gavia "Object of Interest Search" (2022 Terradepth announcement): ML ATR with text query for pipelines, hazards, "man-made debris"; current availability in 2026 unconfirmed — [iConnect007 / Teledyne release](https://iconnect007.com/article/130693/teledyne-marine-announces-sale-of-three-gavia-auvs-to-terradepth/130696/milaero)
- EvoLogics Sonobot: onboard AI object recognition on side-scan and video; pre-trained classes (missing persons, UXO, anchors, wrecks) plus user-trainable; tested by Dutch national police; claims detection in under a second; no accuracy figures found. Based on search snippets only (Hydro International returned 403, evologics.de cert error) — [EvoLogics](https://evologics.de/object-recognition), [Hydro International](https://www.hydro-international.com/content/article/object-recognition-for-side-scan-sonar-and-video-feeds)
- Sonardyne Solstice: ATR lives in MINTACS software (marks mine-like objects, onboard ATR), not in the sonar; Solstice described as suited to CAD/CAC (computer-aided detection/classification) — [ADS Group](https://www.adsgroup.org.uk/knowledge/industry-member-news/sonardyne-s-solstice-mcm-sonar-now-mintacs-compatible-for-ran/)
- Hypack Marine Search / HYSCAN (search-and-recovery oriented): targets marked manually in waterfall/mosaic; real-time mosaic to confirm 100% coverage; target-classification database that assigns a "confidence value" by comparing a new target with user-built example images (older newsletters, 2012-2017); no automatic detection found — [Hypack Marine Search (Officer.com)](https://www.officer.com/tactical/swat/rescue-equipment/product/12116559/hypack-marine-search), [Hypack newsletter on targets in HYSCAN, 2012](https://prod.xylem.com/siteassets/brand/hypack/resources/newsletter/2012/targets-in-hyscan.pdf)
- FarSounder (3D forward-looking navigation sonar): Jan 2026 announcement of innovation funding to integrate AI target recognition (i.e. planned/funded, not shipping) — [Unmanned Systems Technology](https://www.unmannedsystemstechnology.com/2026/01/farsounder-secures-innovation-funding-to-integrate-ai-target-recognition-in-3d-sonar/) (headline/snippet only; page returned 403)
- AquaEye (VodaSafe) handheld AI sonar for drowning searches; Goa Fire Services bought two units; official said it can differentiate bodies from vehicles/debris; vendor claim of hours-to-minutes search; no independent validation found — [Herald Goa](https://www.heraldgoa.in/?p=458483)
- Academic/prototype evidence: Chalmers 2026 master's thesis (Seadragon 2.0 USV, BlueView M900 Mk2, YOLO26n on Jetson Orin Nano) reports precision 0.868, recall 0.812, mAP 0.859 at 39.1 FPS on submerged humans and simulated debris; no false-alarm rate; "theoretical" search coverage 244.3 m2/s; field validation limited — [Chalmers ODR](https://odr.chalmers.se/items/8a7cea57-8075-424f-9159-4174e9e3165d/full). Public dataset: Marine Debris Forward-Looking Sonar Datasets — [arXiv 2503.22880](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2503.22880)
- Coverage tools that exist are geometric/real-time-mosaic displays (Hypack 100% coverage check, Deep Trekker SoundTiles/3D coverage display), not Pd maps — sources above

### Inferences
- Pd-per-cell (search-theory) coverage maps and calibrated, validated per-contact confidence were not found in any civilian debris-search product reviewed. Vendors that say "probability estimates" (Chesapeake) or "confidence value" (Hypack) do not document calibration. This absence is based on web search plus vendor marketing pages; it is not proof that such features are absent in internal naval MCM tools (MCM Pd/clearance-level modeling is a well-known naval practice that I did not research or source here).
- Most ATR products output a candidate list for human confirmation (Kongsberg: "for review/confirmation"), i.e. human-in-the-loop remains the design norm.

### Gaps
- No vendor-published ROC curves, Pd vs false-alarm rate, or independent field trials for any civilian debris product.
- Whether SonarWiz 8.1 ATR is production-grade (conflicting sources) and whether Gavia Object of Interest Search still ships in 2026 are unresolved.
- Garmin/Humminbird, Norbit, Ping DSP, MarineSonic, Hypack current (2025-26) versions, QPS Qimera ATR features: not confirmed.

## 4. Does vendor evidence suggest manual sonar review is still the norm or has been automated?

### Takeaway
Manual review is still the norm, especially in ROV, public-safety and civilian survey workflows; automation is emerging as assistive candidate-list generation (Kongsberg, SonarWiz, AutoTRap, Sonobot, AquaEye) with unproven or undocumented performance.

### Cited Findings
- Deep Trekker page frames sonar use as skill-based ("art", "requires practice"), with the operator choosing scan modes/altitude — [Deep Trekker](https://www.deeptrekker.com/news/sonar-systems)
- Hypack Marine Search: targets marked by hand in waterfall/mosaic — [Officer.com](https://www.officer.com/tactical/swat/rescue-equipment/product/12116559/hypack-marine-search)
- Chesapeake (Feb 2026) describes the status quo as processors reviewing "hundreds of contacts" and correcting bottom tracks/gain manually and positions ML as an improvement still under development — [Maritime Magazines](https://www.maritimemagazines.com/marine-technology/202602/the-future-of-sonar-processing)
- Kongsberg's automation produces a target list "for review/confirmation" — [Kongsberg](https://www.kongsberg.com/news/news-archive/2024/sas-target-assistant/)
- Public-safety side-scan use is long-established and operator-interpreted (DHS technical note 2008, older) — [DHS](https://dhs.gov/sites/default/files/publications/SideScanSonar_TN_0708-508.pdf)

### Inferences
- The gap NetSight-style tooling might address (calibrated confidence, Pd/coverage-completeness, false-alarm control for civilian search) appears real based on this evidence, but absence-of-evidence from web search is not conclusive, and the vendor field is moving (Kongsberg, Chesapeake, FarSounder 2026 announcements).

### Gaps
- No survey data on what fraction of civilian search teams use any automated detection.
