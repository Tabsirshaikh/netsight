## 1. How is marine debris / ghost gear detected and cleaned in India? Is sonar used? Is it manual?

### Takeaway
Ghost-net work in India's best-documented hotspot (Gulf of Mannar) is done by scuba/scientist divers using visual survey methods (roving diver, belt transect), not sonar. Fishing-gear retrieval elsewhere is ad hoc: fisher buy-backs, trawler trials, NGO beach clean-ups. I found no source showing sonar used for ghost-net or routine debris detection in India. Sonar (side-scan) is used in India for wreck/obstruction/debris surveys by the Navy and by private ROV firms, and interpretation appears to be human.

### Cited Findings
- SDMRI's reef debris survey used a "roving diver technique and belt transect method" between Feb 2018 and Mar 2019, i.e. diver-based visual survey. Search-summary only — [Marine Pollution Bulletin abstract (ScienceDirect, 403)](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0025326X1930949X); same details in [FAO AGRIS record](https://agris.fao.org/search/en/records/65de4b4c0f3e94b9e5cd1ed1)
- Removal of nets from coral requires professional divers who take nets off carefully without damaging corals; SDMRI director Patterson Edward says removal should be only by scientist divers, "not novice recreational divers". Opened — [Mongabay-India, Sept 2025](https://india.mongabay.com/2025/09/india-needs-a-marine-litter-policy-as-plastic-waste-chokes-corals/). The article does not describe any detection method and does not mention sonar or remote detection.
- Tamil Nadu Forest Dept / Gulf of Mannar Biosphere Reserve Trust decided to assess debris and prepare an action plan to remove ghost nets, with underwater assessment of 21 islands and adjoining reefs. Search-summary only — [DT Next](https://www.dtnext.in/news/tamilnadu/study-to-assess-impact-of-ghost-nets-on-gulf-of-mannar)
- Forest officials trained in scuba (training by Wildlife Institute of India and forest dept) have taken part in cleanups; one drive had eight scuba divers. Search-summary only — [NewsBytes](https://www.newsbytesapp.com/news/india/tn-forest-officials-remove-plastic-waste-by-scuba-diving/story)
- Earlier SDMRI progress report: 7 island areas in southern Gulf of Mannar surveyed covering 5.21 sq km of reef before removal started. Search-summary only — [UN SDG partnership progress report 2018](https://sdgs.un.org/partnership-progress/partnership-progress-2018-11-16)
- UNDP-backed M. S. Swaminathan Research Foundation project in Tamil Nadu reports 16.2 t of marine debris removed, 12.9 t of it abandoned nets (community-based). Search-summary only (page 403) — [UNDP India](https://www.undp.org/india/stories/protecting-marine-life-how-tamil-nadus-coastal-communities-are-clearing-abandoned-fishing-nets)
- Fishery Survey of India (lead agency for the GloLitter Partnership in India) says it retrieved about 7 tonnes of ghost gear from the Andhra-Odisha coast over three years. WWF-India ran 15 trawler trial runs in May 2023 and "were able to get small bits of discarded nets, but a major part of ghost gear gets settled on the sea floor". Search-summary only — [Mongabay / ICSF-hosted coverage](https://icsf.net/?p=107424)
- WWF-India beach clean-up at Veraval, Gujarat: 120 kg ghost gear among 550 kg collected. Search-summary only — [WWF-India](https://wwfindia.org/news_facts/feature_stories/paving_the_way_for_a_plastic-free_ocean)
- Mongabay (June 2026): India has no systematic way to track, retrieve or recycle lost gear; efforts are piecemeal; a southern Karnataka buy-back lets fishers bring in gear; some incentive schemes were manipulated by fishers staging retrievals, creating verification burdens. The article does not describe detection/mapping and does not mention sonar. Opened — [Mongabay News, June 2026](https://news.mongabay.com/2026/06/indias-fishers-confront-homegrown-ghost-gear-problem/)
- Lakshadweep: olive ridley turtles found entangled in ghost-net conglomerates in Kavaratti lagoon (Apr 2019, Apr 2021), freed by researchers (chance encounters, no systematic survey). Search-summary only — [IOTN](https://iotn.org/iotn36-02-olive-ridley-turtle-entanglement-in-ghost-nets-in-kavaratti-lagoon-lakshadweep-islands-india/)
- Swachh Sagar Surakshit Sagar (MoES coastal clean-up, launched 2022; first edition 3 Jul to 17 Sep 2022, 75 days). NCCR (MoES) monitors beach/coastal litter (monitoring 2018, 2019, 2021). It is a shoreline campaign; no underwater element found. Search-summary only — [Rajya Sabha reply Feb 2023](https://rsdebate.nic.in/bitstream/123456789/740055/1/PQ_259_09022023_U851_p246_p248.pdf); [Down To Earth primer](https://www.downtoearth.org.in/pollution/plastic-waste-woes-a-primer-on-india-s-marine-litter-problem-87059)
- A 2022 Kerala study of 390 fishers found about 21% of gear lost/abandoned/discarded per year (11.6% lost, 7.5% abandoned, 2.3% deliberately discarded), 167.5 kg per vessel per year; seabed snags the most common cause. Opened — [Mongabay News, June 2026](https://news.mongabay.com/2026/06/indias-fishers-confront-homegrown-ghost-gear-problem/)
- A National Marine Litter Policy announced in 2018 reportedly still not finalised (several sources). Search-summary only — [Down To Earth](https://www.downtoearth.org.in/pollution/plastic-waste-woes-a-primer-on-india-s-marine-litter-problem-87059)

### Inferences
- Gulf of Mannar detection is by diver visual survey in shallow reef (coral reef depths), where sonar is of limited relevance; ghost-net removal there is limited by diver availability and survey effort rather than by lack of a sonar. A sonar tool would matter more for the soft-bottom, deeper trawl-ground gear that WWF-India's trawl trials suggest is settling on the seabed (inference).
- No evidence found of an operational, institutional "sonar debris search" programme for ghost gear in India; gap is real but is a gap in practice, not proof of demand.

### Gaps
- GGGI and Olive Ridley Project India-specific detection work: searches returned nothing usable.
- Andaman/Nicobar, Goa and Karnataka diver or sonar-based efforts: not found.
- NIOT/NCCR/MoES programmes using sonar, AUV or ROV specifically for litter or ghost gear: none found.
- Satellite/drone detection of surface debris in India: not searched in depth; nothing cited.
- Indian Navy / Coast Guard underwater debris clean-up drives: not found.

## 2. Documented need for underwater debris/obstruction detection after disasters

### Takeaway
There are real, documented cases where side-scan sonar was used for post-incident debris/wreck search involving India: the MV X-Press Pearl survey (done by an Indian Navy ship, for Sri Lanka, not in Indian waters) and a post-Cyclone Vardah (2016) sunken-debris survey at a south Indian finger jetty by Planys. I found no documented Indian cases for Chennai/Ennore 2017 oil spill, Kerala floods, bridge collapses, or Dwarka/Mumbai harbour wrecks.

### Cited Findings
- After MV X-Press Pearl sank off Colombo (May-June 2021), INS Sarvekshak was deployed on 25 June 2021 at Sri Lanka's request; the aim was debris that threatened mariners and fishers; data supported advisories and later removal. Opened (MEA mission page) and search-summary (PIB) — [CGI Hambantota press release, 2 Jul 2021](https://www.cgihambantota.gov.in/section/press-releases/indian-naval-ship-ins-sarvekshak-completes-800-miles-of-survey-around-mv-xpress-pearl/); [PIB release PRID 1732390 (403, search-summary only)](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=1732390)
- Planys case study: after Cyclone Vardah (Dec 2016), wrecked sunken material of moderate to large size was suspected at a south Indian finger-jetty berth; Planys ROV Beluga with side-scan sonar, GPS, altimeter and depth sensor surveyed up to 10 m either side of the platform, results tabulated and geo-tagged on the Planys Analytics Dashboard; objects seen between piles at 11.3 m depth. The page does not give the port, client, date, object counts, or whether interpretation was manual. Opened — [Planys SONAR Survey case](https://planystech.com/cases/sonar-survey/)
- Planys ROV Beluga used 3D sonar seabed inspection at a southern India port before dredging, and bathymetry survey of a railway bridge pre-monsoon (company LinkedIn posts, search-summary only) — [Planys LinkedIn](https://in.linkedin.com/company/planys)
- Contract for wreck removal for Shipping Corporation of India (RESOLVE) included 3D scanning for obstructions within 500 m of each wreck so the harbour could be dredged. Snippet did not name the port or date, so I could not confirm the location. Search-summary only — [Marine Link](https://ws19.marinelink.com/news/resolve-salvage-removes343563)
- Indian Navy ship INS Sandhayak was reportedly used to search the sea floor for a missing Coast Guard Dornier 228 and detected its beacon signals. Search-summary of Naval News/secondary sources — [Naval News](https://www.navalnews.com/naval-news/2024/02/indian-navy-inducts-new-survey-vessel-with-kongsberg-hugin-auv/)

### Inferences
- Post-disaster demand exists, but is currently served by the Navy survey fleet and a handful of commercial ROV/survey firms using professional side-scan operators.
- The two documented cases where sonar was used for debris were wreck/hard-object search; neither is a ghost-net or soft-debris case.

### Gaps
- Chennai/Ennore 2017 oil spill: no evidence of an underwater debris sonar survey found (one search; only unrelated Ennore dredging/ecology items).
- Kerala floods 2018, bridge collapses, Dwarka/Mumbai harbour wreck clearance, cyclone port channel clearance (Paradip, Visakhapatnam etc.): no primary sources found.
- IWAI: general statement that IWAI uses geospatial technology for hydrographic surveys since 1990 and conducts periodic surveys of NW-1; no IWAI side-scan sonar obstruction/wreck survey found — [Geospatial World](https://geospatialworld.net/article/surveying-and-mapping-of-national-waterways) (search-summary only).

## 3. Verification of proposal claims

### Takeaway
Claim 1 (Sarvekshak) is confirmed. Claim 3 (~2%) is confirmed but is a proportion of gear used and is far below India-specific Kerala data. Claim 2 (37.9%) could not be found and appears wrong or imprecise: the published SDMRI figure is 43.17 +/- 5.48% of reef debris. Claim 4 is supported by secondary sources but not verified from an Indian Navy / NHO primary page.

### Cited Findings
**Claim 1: INS Sarvekshak, 807 miles side-scan sonar, 54 debris, one uncharted wreck — CONFIRMED (with wording nuance)**
- Release text read directly on the Indian High Commission/CGI page: Sarvekshak "progressed 807 miles of Side Scan Sonar survey" and "identified 54 underwater debris and also one ship-wreck"; dated 2 July 2021 (headline says "800 miles"). Opened — [CGI Hambantota](https://www.cgihambantota.gov.in/section/press-releases/indian-naval-ship-ins-sarvekshak-completes-800-miles-of-survey-around-mv-xpress-pearl/)
- PIB version (not openable, 403; search-summary): "54 prominent underwater debris from MV X-Press Pearl and also one additional uncharted wreck"; three search areas; coordinated with Sri Lanka Navy and NARA — [PIB](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=1732390)
- Precision notes: the figure is 807 *miles* of survey track (nautical miles likely, unit not stated in what I read); "uncharted" wording is in the PIB summary, while the other copy says just "one ship-wreck". Interim reports said 558 miles (the survey was not finished then). Survey was in Sri Lankan waters, not Indian. Also, the PIB statement says the 54 objects were "from MV X-Press Pearl" and the proposal should not describe them as all being from other sources. Interim/other coverage: [Daily Mirror](https://dailymirror.lk/amp/front-page/Indian-Naval-ship-detects-54-objects-of-X-Press-Pearl-High-Commission/238-215068); [Lokmat/ANI interim 558 miles](https://www.lokmattimes.com/international/indian-navy-ship-completes-558-miles-survey-around-mv-xpress-pearl)

**Claim 2: SDMRI Gulf of Mannar, nets 37.9% of abandoned-gear litter on coral reefs — NOT FOUND / likely wrong or imprecise**
- Published figure: abandoned fishing nets made up 43.17 +/- 5.48% of marine debris on Gulf of Mannar reefs (Edward et al., Marine Pollution Bulletin 151, 110793, 2020); about 1,152 sq m of reef affected; live corals the substrate in 39.11% of debris cases (note: 39.11% is substrate, a number which could be confused with the claim); 47.56% of corals in contact with debris fragmented, 34% with tissue loss. Search-summary (paper abstract page 403) — [Marine Pollution Bulletin](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0025326X1930949X); [FAO AGRIS](https://agris.fao.org/search/en/records/65de4b4c0f3e94b9e5cd1ed1). Mongabay (opened) gives rounded 43%, 1,152 sq m, 47.56%, 34% — [Mongabay-India](https://india.mongabay.com/2025/09/india-needs-a-marine-litter-policy-as-plastic-waste-chokes-corals/)
- Other related figures, none equal to 37.9%: SDMRI/IUCN/SALT 2023 litter study — ALDFG 74% of litter weight on Indian side of Gulf of Mannar, 40% of total weight across study; about 60% of debris was ghost nets at two Thoothukudi islets; nets 66% of "threat" at rehab sites. Search-summary only — [DT Next](https://www.dtnext.in/news/tamilnadu/nearly-50-per-cent-of-marine-litter-found-in-gulf-of-mannar-is-plastic-study-790554); [Scroll](https://scroll.in/article/984372/in-the-gulf-of-mannar-abandoned-fishing-nets-are-posing-a-threat-to-corals-and-the-marine-ecosystem)
- Two searches for "37.9%" returned no matching source. Precision note: the 43% is a share of all reef debris, not of "abandoned-gear litter". 37.9% may come from another study (e.g. different year, Palk Bay, or a different metric) but I could not find it.

**Claim 3: ~2% of all fishing gear lost annually (Richardson et al. 2022) — CONFIRMED, with caveats**
- Abstract: "nearly 2%" of all fishing gear lost annually; results: 1.82% (+/-0.20%), 95% CI 1.62-2.03%. By type: gillnets 0.81%, purse seine 1.51%, trawl 3.57% (bottom trawl 3.94%), longline mainlines 3.33%, pots/traps 0.74%. Based on interviews with 451 fishers in seven countries plus global effort data; a proportion of gear used, not mass. Opened — [Science Advances 2022 via PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9555783/); CSIRO summary [CSIRO](https://www.csiro.au/en/news/All/News/2022/October/Fishing-gear-lost-at-sea-globally-revealed)
- Caveat: a Kerala study (390 fishers) found about 21% annual loss, over 10 times the global average, so "2%" understates India-specific losses. Opened — [Mongabay News 2026](https://news.mongabay.com/2026/06/indias-fishers-confront-homegrown-ghost-gear-problem/). Richardson's sample did not appear to focus on India (seven countries, not listed in what I read), so applying 2% to India is an assumption.

**Claim 4: Indian Naval Hydrographic Office equipment includes side-scan/AUV — SUPPORTED by secondary sources, not by a primary NHO page**
- IHO national report (c. 2012-13): Indian Naval Hydrographic Department, with the National Hydrographic Office at Dehradun as sole national chart authority, operates about eight ocean-going survey ships plus a catamaran, and its AUVs are fitted with side-scan sonar. Search-summary only — [IHO NIOHC13 India National Report](https://legacy.iho.int/mtg_docs/rhc/NIOHC/NIOHC13/NIOHC%2013-6-1-15%20-%20India%20National%20Report.pdf)
- Survey Vessel Large (Sandhayak class) ships carry multibeam echo sounders, AUV, ROV and side-scan sonars; Kongsberg HUGIN AUV and HiPAP 502 positioning mentioned. Search-summary only — [Naval News, Feb 2024](https://www.navalnews.com/naval-news/2024/02/indian-navy-inducts-new-survey-vessel-with-kongsberg-hugin-auv/)
- INS Ikshak (2025): AUV capable of multibeam soundings, side-scan imaging, sub-bottom profiling. Search-summary only — [Onmanorama, Nov 2025](https://www.onmanorama.com/news/kerala/2025/11/06/ins-ikshak-hydrographic-survey-vessel.html)
- Sarvekshak's own X-Press Pearl work (claim 1) is direct evidence of side-scan sonar on an Indian survey ship.

### Inferences
- Claims 1 and 3 can be used as written with the precision notes. Claim 2 should be corrected to 43% (43.17 +/- 5.48%) of reef debris from Edward et al. 2020, unless the author can produce the 37.9% source.
- Do not describe Navy survey assets as available for civil debris search; they are tasked for charting and defence.

### Gaps
- Original source of 37.9% not found.
- No primary Indian Navy / NHO / hydrobharat.gov.in page opened for equipment inventory.
- PIB page could not be opened directly (403); content reconstructed from search summary plus the identical CGI Hambantota release.

## 4. Indian startups/institutes building AI sonar / underwater detection

### Takeaway
Defence labs (DRDO NSTL) are building AI-based side-scan sonar target recognition for mine-like objects; Planys Technologies is the main civil underwater robotics firm doing sonar survey for ports/dams/bridges, but I found no evidence it uses ML on sonar for debris, and nothing for NIOT, IIT Madras, IISc beyond Planys's IIT Madras origin. No Indian product specifically for ghost-net or debris sonar detection was found.

### Cited Findings

Search-summary only — [The Week](https://www.theweek.in/news/defence/2025/11/14/deep-sea-defence-upgrade-drdo-develops-ai-enabled-mine-hunter-for-indian-navy-what-can-it-do.html); [News Arena](https://www.newsarenaindia.com/nation/drdo-s-new-man-portable-auvs-use-ai-sonar-for-mine-detection/62213)

Source looks auto-generated; low reliability — [Mammoth Times](https://business.mammothtimes.com/mammothtimes/article/tokenring-2025-11-7-silent-sentinels-iiit-a-and-nstl-pioneer-ai-for-advanced-underwater-object-identification)

### Inferences
- The AI-on-sonar capability that exists in India is concentrated in defence; a civil debris/ghost-net-focused AI sonar tool appears to be an open niche (based on absence of evidence in a limited search).

### Gaps
- Sagar Defence, Aquaconnect (aquaculture analytics, probably not relevant), Marine Robotics, Tideworks, IIT Madras and IISc sonar-AI groups, and NIOT OMe-AUV: not found in search.
- Planys's AI/ML claims are from promotional or press sources; none verified.
- Dates on several results unreliable.

## Overall assessment: is sonar-based underwater debris search a real operational need in India?
(Inference, not a cited finding.) The problem (ghost gear, 21% loss in a Kerala study; 43% of debris on Gulf of Mannar reefs; post-cyclone and post-wreck debris) is well documented. However, the documented operational response is diver-based (reefs), fisher buy-back and beach clean-up, and professional side-scan survey by the Navy or contractors for wrecks/ports. I found no evidence that agencies currently request or fund sonar-based ghost-net search, and Mongabay (June 2026) says India lacks even a tracking system. So the need is plausible but not shown as an expressed operational requirement.
