# What we learned on 2026-11-08 (research model work)

Plain-language summary. Details and all numbers: the linked documents in this folder.

## The model

- **Research model v0.4** (`runs/research_v0.4/model`; D-FINE-S, five datasets, weight averaging).
  Labelled "research model" because it learned from research-only data (SCTD). Never sold as is.
- Scores on surveys it never saw (AP50, 0-1): SCTD 0.82, AquaScan 0.61, GhostVision 0.36,
  Contact (other sensor) 0.26, mine 0.22, SubPipe 0.18 (0.68 with a looser box match). Average
  0.42; v0.1 was ~0.27 and v0.2 ~0.36. (research_model_v0.4.md)
- **GhostVision specialist** (`runs/ft_v0.4_ghostvision/model`): best on ghost gear, Contact 0.40.
  (finetune_v0.4.md)
- Papers report 75-99% because they test on random images from surveys the model has seen.
  Scored that way, our pipeline matches them (0.72 vs the paper's 75% on the mine dataset); on
  unseen surveys the same model scores 0.21. (replication.md, literature_pipelines.md)

## What worked

1. **Balancing datasets** (v0.2) and **weight averaging** (v0.4): real gains, confirmed with
   paired confidence intervals on the same test images.
2. **Flip test-time augmentation**: +0.02-0.04 AP for free (tta_v0.1.md).
3. **Correcting pixel geometry per survey**: the other-sensor recording went 0.175 -> 0.368 with
   no retraining (test_b_resampling.md).
4. **More surveys**: GhostVision rises 0.26 -> 0.34 -> 0.37 with 5 -> 10 -> 21 recordings, and
   the other-sensor recording gains most. Collecting varied surveys pays off
   (test_a_learning_curve.md).

## What did not work (and why)

1. **Zoom augmentation** (v0.3) and the **mine fine-tune**: better on validation, worse on test.
   Our validation sets are too small (mine: 30 objects) to choose between models; only paired
   test comparisons decide.
2. **A false-alarm threshold fixed in advance**: broken on most new surveys, because false-alarm
   rates vary a lot between surveys (operating_point_v0.1.md, trust_v0.4.md).

## The trust layer

- Raw detector scores are overconfident; calibration makes them honest on every test set.
- **False alarms are kept within budget by counting on the survey itself** (flag at most N per
  image or per km2): held on every held-out survey, no labels needed. Recall is then reported as
  an estimate with its interval.
- **Geometry check**: detects when a survey's pixels look different from training (Contact,
  mine 2015) and marks results "indicative" instead of "calibrated".
- **Evidence packs** (JSON + HTML, hash-chained ledger) for four held-out surveys:
  `netsight-data/runs/demo_v0.4`. Built by `python -m netsight.demo.run_survey`.

## Data

- 60 candidate chips around NOAA-verified objects from three NOAA side-scan mosaics
  (`processed/noaa_chips`). 1 m pixels: large objects only; each box needs a human check.
- Labelling convention proposed (object only, shadow excluded), with each public dataset's
  convention listed: `docs/labelling_guide.md` section 4.1. Team decision needed.

## Next steps (v5)

1. **Data first** (test A says it pays): team reviews the NOAA chips; send the data-request
   emails (`docs/outreach/data_requests.md`); look for 5-10 cm side-scan data with metadata.
2. **Pre-labelling tool**: our model + SAM2 proposes boxes in X-AnyLabeling/CVAT; people accept,
   adjust or reject (seconds per image).
3. **Resampling module**: convert every survey to the training pixel size and shape using its
   sonar metadata; the geometry gate catches the rest.
4. **Bigger validation sets** from the new surveys, so model choices stop depending on luck.
5. Then train v5 and compare it with v0.4 on the same frozen test surveys.
